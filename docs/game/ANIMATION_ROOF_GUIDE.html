What changed, in one picture

Before: four rooms that all spoke motion but never to each other. The five clips only played on the exact 24-bone canonical skeleton, so your SkinTokens player (M4004) stood rigid in /play. Motion capture drove a live preview and recorded nothing — every pose evaporated when the session closed. There was no way to author a keyframe at all.

After: a body is just something that answers to roles (pelvis, upper-arm-left, …). Motion — captured, hand-keyed, or migrated from the clips — is a document of role-addressed poses at declared times. One mixer plays documents onto bodies, layered by role. Pose what you care about, declare when, and the system fills in the rest — on any character, from any source, saved to disk forever.

The five steps that landed

Numbered because the order is the dependency chain — each step assumed the one before it.

1

Roles became the motion vocabulary.

Clips now sample as role-addressed deltas (humanoid_clips.zig · CHANNEL_IDS, sampleChannels). The old gate — “exactly 24 bones, exact ID order, or you get bind pose forever” — is dead. A body answers to the clips when it binds the 12 clip roles; M4004’s 13 role-bound joints qualify. This is the change that un-freezes the rigid player (req_4279).

2

The motion document (RJAN v1) and the recording tap.

One durable format (motion_document.zig): sparse keys where you were sparse, dense runs where a take was dense. Capture sessions gained record / recordStop — a stopped take lands on disk as motion-<sha256>.rjan and is immediately replayable on a different body. Capture finally attributes to something.

3

The five clips migrated into documents.

clip_documents.zig generates idle/walk/jump/sit/lay from the tuning table, so the two can never drift. Playback parity was swept in-suite (5 clips × 25 sample times × canonical and adopted bodies), then shot-verified byte-identical per clip — and the source of truth flipped: the game now plays the five from their document form; the table remains as their generator (see gates).

4

The mixer.

Up to four motion layers compose over the clip floor by role coverage. Mounting blends in over 0.15 s; stopping snapshots the layer’s last pose and fades it out over 0.35 s — the same hold/fade discipline the capture gate already used for dropouts. A full-body external owner still outranks everything, so the ownership doors behave identically from the host’s view.

5

The workbench dock.

A MOTION strip under the animation surface’s three capture panes: record takes, open them as editable documents, add keys from the captured pose, scrub, re-time, and play the result on the /play player. Everything below walks through it.

Concepts worth knowing

TermWhat it means
Role channelA semantic body part — pelvis, spine_lower, upper_arm_left, … 15 in the driven set. Motion addresses these, never a rig’s private bone names, which is why one motion fits every body.
Motion documentThe saved artifact (.rjan). Holds keys and runs over some set of role channels, plus a name, duration, and loop flag.
KeyA pose at a declared time. Keys are partial by nature: a key may pose one arm and say nothing about the rest — untouched roles keep doing whatever they were doing.
RunA dictated dense range — capture’s native tongue. Every recorded frame, with its real timing preserved. Shown as a green bar on the timeline.
LayerA slot in the mixer (0–3). Layer 0 replaces the clip floor; higher layers compose partial documents on top by role coverage.
Fill-inBetween two keys the system interpolates (shortest-arc slerp, or smooth/hold easing per key). Joint limits are clamped every frame downstream, so an in-between can never bend an elbow backwards.

Before anything: one rebuild

Required

Every piece of this lives in framework/ Zig, so hot reload does not carry it. One fresh build of the editor (your normal ship/dev-native loop) lights up all of it at once: the un-frozen player, the record button, the doors, the dock. Until then the dock renders but its buttons refuse with “no door” errors.

First proof

After the rebuild, just open /play. M4004 should walk, idle, jump, sit, and lay instead of gliding rigidly — no settings, no migration, nothing to convert. That is step 1 working.

Guide · record a motion take

The animation surface (the capture workbench with the three panes) now has a MOTION strip along the bottom.

1

Open the animation surface with a saved character selected, camera running, and let calibration finish — same as any capture session. The character mirrors you in the target pane.

2

Press REC TAKE. The button flips to STOP REC · n and counts frames as you move. Everything the capture drives is being kept now.

3

Press STOP REC. The take is written to disk (content-addressed, so re-saving the identical take is free) and opens on the timeline as a green run bar — instantly scrubbable and replayable.

Note

Recalibrating or flipping the depth sign mid-take discards the recording on purpose — a take cannot span two different rest references. Dropped frames (if the machine is starved) are reported as TRUNCATED on the receipt, never swallowed.

Guide · author keyframes by hand

The dream loop: strike a pose, declare when it happens, forget the rest.

1

Press NEW DOC. You get an empty 2-second looping document over the full role set.

2

Move the playhead: press anywhere on the SCRUB tick track, or fine-step with −/+.

3

Strike the pose in front of the camera (freeze the triplet if you want to nail it precisely), then press ADD KEY @ PLAYHEAD. The captured pose lands as an amber key diamond at that time. Capture is the pose input — the highest-bandwidth one we have. With no capture running, the button adds a neutral bind key instead.

4

Repeat at other times. Three keys make a motion. The keys bracketing the playhead glow green — that’s your ghosting: what you’re blending from and to at this instant.

5

Select any key to re-time it: ⟨ NUDGE / NUDGE ⟩ steps it 0.05 s, MOVE TO PLAYHEAD plants it exactly where you’re parked, DELETE removes it. Sliding keys around never breaks anything — the in-betweens recalculate on the next sample.

6

SAVE writes it to the motion library by name.

Guide · scrub, re-time, play on /play

The timeline shows one row per role group — TORSO, ARM L, ARM R, LEG L, LEG R — so a partial key (say, arm-only) appears only on the rows it actually poses.

1

Have the /play surface open somewhere — it registers itself as the playback target. The dock’s status line tells you when no /play world is available.

2

Press PLAY on /play. The document is saved to disk first (the mixer plays disk truth, never an unsaved phantom) and mounted on the embodied player. Press again — STOP /play — and the player fades back to its clips over a third of a second.

3

While mounted, pressing the scrub track parks the live player at that exact frame — authoring is owed the exact pose, so scrubbing bypasses the blend ramp. RESUME releases it back into playback from wherever you parked.

Guide · layering — wave over walk

Because channels are per-role, a partial document composes over whatever plays underneath. A wave document that only covers arm roles, mounted on layer 1, waves — while the walk keeps the legs, the hips, and the root bob. Whoever owns a role, owns it; everything else is untouched.

The dock plays on layer 0 (full preview). Layered playback is a door call away for carts and agents:

// wave (arm-only document) over whatever the world is doing:
__compiled_world_play_motion(nodeId, "userdata/editor/motion/wave.rjan", 1);

// stop just that layer — the wave fades out, the walk was never interrupted:
__compiled_world_play_motion(nodeId, "", 1);
Law

A live capture session or host pose stream still owns the whole body and outranks every layer — exactly the ownership rule the slice always had. Nothing about the host doors changed.

Authoring JSON, by example

What op:"save" accepts and op:"load" returns. A key’s channels map is its coverage — name only what you pose.

{ "name": "wave-hello", "looping": true, "durationSeconds": 1.5,
  "channels": ["upper_arm_left", "lower_arm_left"],
  "keys": [
    { "timeSeconds": 0.0,
      "channels": { "upper_arm_left": [0, 0, 0, 1] } },
    { "timeSeconds": 0.75, "easing": "smooth",
      "root": [0, 0.05, 0],
      "channels": { "upper_arm_left":  [0.38, 0, 0, 0.92],
                    "lower_arm_left":  [0, 0, 0.20, 0.98] } }
  ] }

Quaternions are bind-relative deltas (x, y, z, w) — “how far from rest”, which is exactly why the same file fits every body. Easing per key: slerp (default), smooth, or hold. A planted list annotates ground contact for the future contact/IK layer — recorded now, solved later.

What is still gated

  • Retiring the clip table — DONE (req_4294). The gate ran and passed: each of the five clips was rendered headless both ways (rjit shot — the app photographing its own rendered frame; nothing to do with cameras or motion capture) and every pair came out byte-identical, including a mid-stride sample between authored keys. Three repro hooks made the shots exact: RJIT_FORCE_CLIP pins which clip plays, RJIT_FORCE_CLIP_SECONDS pins the instant, and RJIT_FIXED_DT pins the world clock so the camera lands identically every run. With the gate green, the game now plays idle/walk/jump/sit/lay from their document form — the same format and the same playback path as everything you record or author in the dock. The hand-written tuning table still exists, but as the generator of those documents (and a diagnostic: RJIT_CLIP_SOURCE=table replays the old sampler for comparison). Nothing looks different — that was the point of the gate. What changed is what a clip is: new clips become files you save, not code someone writes.
  • Runtime proofs — M4004 walking in /play is user-confirmed; still awaiting eyes: the facing fix (req_4291), capture driving M4004 live, and the dock end-to-end. Every mechanism behind them is proven in the test suites (test-player-character-pose, test-humanoid-clips, test-motion-document, test-motion-document-json, test-capture-session, test-retarget — all green).
  • Deliberately out of scope, per the proposal: foot planting / IK (the planted annotation is recorded for it), the V6 animation language on top of documents, and the “snappy / heavy” feel-word easing vocabulary.
