# Angle 07 — Joint Placement / Skeleton Inference

Scope: inferring joint/skeleton positions from an unrigged mesh, separate from weighting.

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): Pinocchio (Baran & Popović, SIGGRAPH 2007) is two independent algorithms: skeleton EMBEDDING (discrete + continuous optimization fitting a given template into a graph built over packed medial-surface spheres) and skin attachment (bone heat). The paper's Section 3 (embedding) makes no use of Section 4 (weights); a tool can adopt either half alone.
- Claim (high): Pinocchio's embedding pipeline is: rescale mesh to a unit cube → adaptive signed distance field on an octree (tolerance τ = 0.003) → approximate medial surface as points on octree cell faces where adjacent-cell gradients differ by ≥ 120°, discarding points within 2τ of the surface → greedy sphere packing (largest-radius medial points first; add a sphere iff its center lies outside all previous spheres, radius = distance to surface) → graph with edges between intersecting spheres plus "essential" Gabriel-graph edges → embed a REDUCED template (degree-2 joints like knees/elbows merged out; humanoid s = 18 joints reduces to r = 7) by minimizing a penalty over r-tuples of graph vertices.
- Claim (high): Pinocchio's discrete search is branch-and-bound with a priority queue of partial embeddings ordered by lower bounds — the paper calls it "essentially the A* algorithm on the tree of possible embeddings"; the first complete embedding popped is optimal. The penalty is a linear combination of k = 9 hand-built basis penalties (short bones; improper joint orientation; asymmetric lengths for bones marked symmetric; bone chains sharing vertices; "feet" far from the bottom; zero-length chains; improper bone orientation; degree-1 joints not at extreme vertices; joints far along chains but close in the graph), with weights learned by a maximum-margin (SVM-inspired) procedure over 62 characters / ~400 example embeddings, optimized with Nelder–Mead.
- Claim (high): Pinocchio's stated assumptions are (a) the mesh is the boundary of a single connected volume, (b) the character is in approximately the same orientation AND pose as the template, and (c) proportioned roughly like it. Measured failure modes: 13/16 unseen test characters embedded correctly (81%), the other 3 fixed with one manual joint hint; limbs thinner than radius 2τ disconnect the sphere graph; and the authors state for interior degree-2 joints (knees/elbows): "We do not know of a reliable way to identify the right locations for them."
- Claim (high): Pinocchio's runtime was dominated by discretization, not search: mean 31.3 s/character (24.3 s distance field + medial surface, 5.2 s discrete embedding, ~1.2 s continuous refinement) on a 2007-era 1.73 GHz Core Duo, single-threaded — interactive-adjacent, not realtime, even scaled to modern hardware.
- Claim (high): Au et al., "Skeleton Extraction by Mesh Contraction" (SIGGRAPH 2008) produces a curve skeleton with NO template: iterative implicit Laplacian smoothing with global positional (attraction) constraints collapses the mesh to near-zero volume without changing connectivity; "connectivity surgery" (edge collapses) then reduces it to a 1D skeleton; a refinement step re-centers nodes using the induced skeleton–mesh mapping. It also outputs skeleton–vertex correspondence and local thickness; the paper demonstrates segmentation and skinning animation.
- Claim (high): Tagliasacchi et al., "Mean Curvature Skeletons" (SGP 2012) reformulates contraction as mean curvature flow guided toward the medial axis via Voronoi poles (input is remeshed to get usable poles), and this exact algorithm ships as CGAL's Triangulated Surface Mesh Skeletonization package (`CGAL::extract_mean_curvature_flow_skeleton()` / `Mean_curvature_flow_skeletonization`). CGAL requires a boundaryless, single-component triangulated mesh, needs Eigen, and returns a `boost::adjacency_list` whose vertices carry a 3D point plus their contracted input vertices (~5.6 s @ 44,460 vertices). ROSA (Tagliasacchi et al. 2009) is the point-cloud sibling (handles incomplete scans).
- Claim (high): a raw curve skeleton is not an animation skeleton. Concretely: nodes are dense samples, not articulation joints; junctions sit where branches merge geometrically, not at anatomical pivots; there are no semantics (no "elbow"), no root, no orientation conventions; and animator practice deliberately deviates from centeredness — RigNet's authors observe that "character spines are often created closer to the back rather than the medial surface or centerline, mimicking human and animal anatomy." Conversion needs joint sparsification, junction cleanup, root selection, and template matching for semantics.
- Claim (medium): the best-documented geometric→animation conversions are (a) Aujay et al., "Harmonic Skeleton for Realistic Character Animation" (SCA 2007): Reeb graph of a harmonic function seeded from ONE user-picked point on the head, guaranteed free of spurious endpoints, with joints then associated to anatomy for humanoids/quadrupeds; and (b) Bharaj et al., "Automatically Rigging Multi-component Characters" (Eurographics 2012): for multi-part game models, build a contact graph of rigid components, cluster it into a skeleton, then map a target animation skeleton (with motion) onto it, tolerating scale/proportion and some morphological differences without labels.
- Claim (high): RigNet (Xu, Zhou, Kalogerakis, Landreth, Singh — SIGGRAPH/TOG 2020) predicts joints by (1) a GNN (GMEdgeNet: edge convolutions over BOTH one-ring neighborhoods and geodesic balls of radius 0.06× the model's longest dimension) regressing per-vertex displacements toward candidate joints, (2) a same-architecture per-vertex attention network, and (3) attention-modulated differentiable mean-shift clustering (Epanechnikov kernel; bandwidth learned by backprop to h = 0.057; exposed as a 0.01–0.1 level-of-detail slider). Displaced points and attention are mirrored across the global bilateral symmetry plane before clustering. Connectivity: BoneNet pairwise bone probabilities → Prim's MST (edge cost −log p), rooted by RootNet (distance-to-symmetry-plane is a feature).
- Claim (high): RigNet's stated sole input assumption is "a consistent upright and frontfacing orientation"; it was trained on ModelsResource-RigNetv1 — 2,703 rigged characters (humanoids, quadrupeds, birds, fish, robots, toys), deduplicated at >95% voxel IoU, split 2,163/270/270, meshes normalized to 1K–5K vertices, 3–48 joints (mean 25.0). On its test set it scored IoU 61.6% / CD-J2J 3.9% vs Pinocchio's 36.5% / 7.2% (Pinocchio run with automatic template selection among humanoid, short quadruped, tall quadruped, centaur). Self-reported limitations: no guaranteed invariance to tessellation/resolution, occasional spurious extra joints, and it cannot predict helper bones its dataset lacks (fingers, feet, clothing).
- Claim (medium): Mixamo's Auto-Rigger is a guided-landmark system: Adobe's official doc instructs placing markers on "wrists, elbows, knees, and groin" following on-screen instructions, states rigging takes "a few minutes," and that it "only works for humanoid characters with distinguishable head, body, arm, and leg areas." The chin marker and mirrored placement (moving the left elbow moves the right) are confirmed by Adobe community/Fuse-era guidance, giving ~8 markers total (chin, 2 wrists, 2 elbows, 2 knees, groin). The algorithm behind it is proprietary and unpublished — nothing beyond the marker workflow is documented; its internals would be speculation.
- Claim (high): for humanoid scans, fitting a parametric body model gives joints for free: SMPL (Loper et al., SIGGRAPH Asia 2015) learns, among its parameters, "a regressor from vertices to joint locations," so once SMPL is registered to a scan the 24-joint skeleton is a learned sparse linear function of the fitted surface. This is category-locked: it does not extend to arbitrary creatures.
- Claim (medium): the 2025 generation reframes joint placement as autoregressive sequence generation over skeleton trees: RigAnything (TOG/SIGGRAPH 2025) emits joints in BFS order as (3D position via diffusion head, parent index), template-free, trained on RigNet + Objaverse rigs, "under a few seconds per shape"; UniRig (2025) introduces Skeleton Tree Tokenization plus bone-point cross-attention skinning, trained on its Rig-XL dataset (>14K rigged models), claiming 215%/194% rigging/motion accuracy improvements; Anymate (2025) contributes a 230K-asset rigging dataset ("70× larger than existing") with a joint/connectivity/skinning baseline bake-off. All figures are self-reported; independent replication not surveyed.
- Claim (medium): for a general-purpose tool where users build ARBITRARY creatures, the defensible architecture is template-free geometric skeletonization (CGAL MCF skeleton) as the always-works fallback, an optional Mixamo-style marker pass or template embedding for humanoids, and optionally a learned model (RigNet-lineage or 2025 autoregressive) where shipping weights/runtime is acceptable. Template embedding alone fails by construction off-template (Pinocchio's measured 36.5% IoU on diverse characters); raw skeletonization alone leaves joint semantics unsolved.

## Technical detail

**Family 1 — Template embedding (Pinocchio).** Discretization: signed distance field on an octree (cells split until interpolation error < τ = 0.003 on a unit-cube-normalized mesh); medial-surface samples where gradients across cell faces disagree by ≥ 120° (intentionally discarding "noisy" medial sheets); greedy sphere packing over those samples; graph = intersecting-sphere edges ∪ essential edges (edge must stay ≥ ½·min-radius inside the surface, and its endpoints must be the closest centers to its midpoint — equivalent to requiring Gabriel-graph membership). All-pairs shortest paths are precomputed to price penalties fast. Reduction: only branch joints of the template are embedded discretely; degree-2 joints are reinserted afterward by splitting graph shortest paths proportionally to the template — which is exactly why knees/elbows land badly (limb interiors are featureless in the graph). Refinement: continuous minimization of g = α_A·(asymmetry) + Σᵢ gᵢ where gᵢ = α_S·(bone-fits-inside-surface) + α_L·(short-bone) + α_O·(orientation-vs-template), four hand-picked α's, gradient descent with step-doubling line search, ~10 outer iterations. Manual hints ("this joint must be at vertex v") prune the search and fixed all observed failures. Template attributes (symmetric/feet marks) live with the skeleton, so new templates are cheap; RigNet's harness auto-selects among four templates by fitting cost — a practical recipe.

**Family 2 — Geometric skeletonization.** Au et al. 2008: solve (per iteration) a weighted linear system mixing the contraction constraint L·V′ = 0 (cotangent Laplacian) with attraction constraints anchoring vertices near their current positions; re-weight each iteration (contraction weight up as area collapses); stop when volume ≈ 0; then collapse edges of degenerate faces (a QEM-style cost with a shape-preservation term) to get a 1D graph; re-center nodes using which mesh vertices collapsed into them. Works directly on the mesh (no voxels), so resolution-independent in spirit; needs a reasonable triangulation and behaves best on watertight single-component inputs. MCF Skeletons 2012 recasts this as medially-guided mean curvature flow with explicit degeneracy detection and local remeshing (angle split + edge contraction each iteration) — this is the version to use, because CGAL maintains it with a two-call API and documented parameters (`max_triangle_angle`, `quality_speed_tradeoff`, `medially_centered_speed_tradeoff`, `is_medially_centered`). The output correspondence (skeleton node → set of contracted surface vertices) is the load-bearing artifact for a rigging tool: it gives you both an initial skin-binding partition and per-node thickness. Conversion to an animation skeleton then requires: prune micro-branches (thickness/length ratio), collapse near-collinear runs into single bones, place joints at curvature maxima of limb polylines (the weakest documented step — see unsure), pick a root (RigNet found animators don't always root at the centroid-closest joint), and label semantically only by matching a template graph (Aujay et al. do this anatomically; Pinocchio's related work argues embedding beats extraction precisely because extraction's topology varies — "ears may be mistaken for arms").

**Family 3 — Learned / landmark.** RigNet details worth copying even without the network: (a) displace-then-cluster beats direct regression (their P2PNet-based ablation drops IoU from 61.6% to 40.6%); (b) attention pretraining matters (no-pretrain: 60.6%); (c) one-ring-only graph conv hurts (59.3%) — the geodesic-ball neighborhoods carry it; (d) Euclidean-distance MST vs learned bone probabilities: 61.2% vs 83.7% connectivity classification accuracy — i.e., "connect nearest joints" is a genuinely bad topology prior; (e) explicit symmetrization by reflection is done in data space, pre-clustering, not in the network. Mixamo: markers → proprietary fitting; docs promise humanoids only; single clean mesh recommended; FBX/OBJ/ZIP upload. SMPL-family fitting: register the model to the scan (correspondence + pose/shape optimization), read joints from the learned regressor; robust for humans/mocap-adjacent tools, useless for a dragon. 2025 autoregressive models (RigAnything, UniRig) drop the fixed-cluster assumption entirely — the skeleton is a token sequence, so joint COUNT and topology are sampled, not clustered; both claim cross-category generality (anime characters, quadrupeds, marine creatures, insects).

**Practical priors (any family).** Extremities: geodesic-farthest points ("tree diameter" — the two most geodesically distant vertices land on wrist-tip-like extremities; iterate farthest-point sampling for more limbs) are the standard hands/feet/head seed, formalized as "prominent feature points" by Katz, Leifman & Tal (2005) and used by Tierny et al. (PG 2006); Pinocchio encodes the same prior as a penalty (degree-1 joints at extreme vertices). Symmetry: RigNet assumes a global bilateral plane from consistent orientation; for unoriented input you must detect it — extrinsic reflective symmetry via transform-space voting (Mitra et al. 2006; surveys exist), intrinsic symmetry via geodesic-preserving self-maps (e.g., arXiv:1807.10162) for posed meshes. Canonical pose: every family cheats off it — Pinocchio requires template-like pose/orientation, RigNet requires upright+front-facing, Mixamo wants a T/A-pose humanoid; a tool for arbitrary creatures should either enforce a canonical-pose convention at import or restrict itself to pose-free machinery (curve skeletons + intrinsic symmetry).

## What I'm unsure about

- Curve-skeleton → animation-joint conversion has no single canonical published recipe; Aujay 2007 (anatomy labeling) and Bharaj 2012 (multi-component) are the closest primary sources, but the specific "place a knee at the curvature maximum of a limb polyline" step in most pipelines is folklore/engineering, not a citable algorithm. Pinocchio explicitly says interior degree-2 joint localization is unsolved geometrically; I found no later geometric paper that convincingly closes this.
- Mixamo: the chin marker and mirroring are sourced from community/product-UI discussions, not the current Adobe help page (which lists wrists/elbows/knees/groin); Adobe pages timed out on direct fetch, so I could not verify the Skeleton LOD bone-count options (e.g., "no fingers" variants) from the official doc. The auto-rigger's internal algorithm is fully proprietary — nothing published.
- The `penalty.pdf` supplemental link is the 2007 URL cited in the paper; I did not verify it still resolves.
- 2025 autoregressive methods (RigAnything, UniRig, Anymate): all quantitative claims are self-reported from abstracts/project pages; I found no independent benchmark reproducing them, and licensing of Objaverse-derived rig training data for a commercial tool is unexamined here.
- Katz–Leifman–Tal 2005 ("feature point and core extraction," The Visual Computer 21(8)) is cited from search-confirmed bibliographic data; I did not fetch a stable PDF URL for it.
- I did not deep-verify MagicArticulate / ARMO / MoRig (2022–2025 adjacent works); they exist in the citation graph but are omitted rather than characterized loosely.
