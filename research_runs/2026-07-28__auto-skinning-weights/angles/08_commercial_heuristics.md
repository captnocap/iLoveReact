# Angle 08 — Commercial Auto-Rigger Heuristics

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): The premise is correct and is *documented in Mixamo's own patent*: Mixamo's uploaded-mesh auto-rigger computes skinning weights by heat diffusion where each body segment is a heat emitter solved as an independent Poisson problem, and — critically — uses "problem specific diffusion coefficients and Dirichlet-type boundary conditions ... to capture the peculiarity of each segment/joint," with those parameters "automatically estimated ... using machine learning techniques applied on a training set of manually skinned models," producing "skinning templates that are specific for a given joint" (US 8,797,328 B2, Mixamo Inc., priority 2010, granted 2014). "Shoulder-aware" is not folklore; it is literally claimed as per-joint learned diffusion parameters.
- Claim (high): The same patent documents the full Mixamo pipeline: (1) build a single closed-form proxy mesh from the user's possibly-multi-part input (visual hull or "ray targeting"); (2) find salient points (head top, armpits, fingers, groin, feet) via Laplacian eigenmaps / medial axis / PCA regression on a training set *or manually via a UI* — which is exactly the public marker-placement screen; (3) fit a reference skeleton using statistical joint-location priors plus Pinocchio-style graph embedding (Baran & Popović 2007 is cited by name inside the patent); (4) solve weights on the proxy; (5) transfer skeleton + weights back to the original mesh(es) by mean-value coordinates or nearest-neighbor + local interpolation. This proxy-then-transfer design is why Mixamo tolerates multi-mesh, non-watertight uploads.
- Claim (high): Adobe's current Mixamo help documents the user-facing contract: markers placed on "wrists, elbows, knees, and groin" (the live UI adds a chin marker — third-party tutorials show it), server-side rigging in minutes, bipedal humanoids only, and a constraint list (distinguishable head/body/arm/leg regions, near-neutral symmetric pose, no large extra appendages, no floating disconnected parts, character centered, clean single-file mesh). No polygon cap is stated in current docs. The "no spaces between any of the parts" rule is consistent with (and credibly explained by) the patent's closed-form-proxy step.
- Claim (high): Mixamo's *other* documented strategy is pure template weight transfer: for its parametric character generator, US 9,305,387 B2 describes a PCA generative body model (built on SCAPE/Anguelov-style correspondence) whose outputs share polygon correspondence with artist-rigged "clothing mesh templates," so skinning weights are *copied verbatim* from the hand-skinned template and only the template skeleton joints are re-fitted (via mean value coordinates). I.e., where Mixamo controls the topology, it does not solve weights at all — it reuses artist weights.
- Claim (high): Autodesk Maya documents four bind weight initializers by name — Closest Distance, Closest in Hierarchy, Heat Map (joints as heat sources), Geodesic Voxel — and Autodesk's own help states that "legacy bind methods create artifacts, and Heat Map binding often fails on real-world meshes," which Geodesic Voxel fixes by voxelizing the character and computing weights on the voxel grid (published method: Dionne & de Lasa, "Geodesic voxel binding for production character meshes," SCA 2013). Per-joint control is documented as: Max Influences (default 5), a Dropoff/Falloff rate, and Interactive Bind's per-joint capsule/cylinder volume manipulators.
- Claim (high): Reallusion documents (as a product claim, not an algorithm) that AccuRIG's weights are joint-specialized: "The skin-weight is highly optimized for all limbs, including shoulders, elbows, knees, and individual finger bones," with dedicated marketing sections for Shoulder / Neck / Finger, square-vs-sloped shoulder handling via relative clavicle–shoulder joint placement, and "volumes for the head, body, and accessories are well-preserved." The actual solver is completely undocumented — no whitepaper, no manual section, and a Google Patents assignee search for Reallusion surfaces no auto-rigging/skinning patents.
- Claim (high): AccuRIG's documented multi-mesh strategy is classification before weighting: in Character Creator's Advanced AccuRIG you "designate specific items such as the body and cloth as the target for auto skin weight" while "hard surface items like helmets or armor" are "auto-attached to neighboring bones as accessories, preventing deformations" (i.e., rigid single-bone binding for hard parts, smooth solve only for deformables), plus bone masking, partial rigging of asymmetric models, and re-rig from an existing skeleton preserving facial bones.
- Claim (medium): AccuRIG inserts twist bones automatically. Practitioner evidence: a Reallusion forum user reports AccuRIG output "ends up with a bunch of additional 'cc twist' bones" even when exporting with the Unreal preset; the CC-standard skeleton AccuRIG targets contains twist bones. Inferred, not documented as an algorithmic statement by Reallusion.
- Claim (medium): Mixamo does *not* insert twist/helper joints: its documented rig options are only a "Skeleton LOD" dropdown (Standard 65 bones down to No Fingers, 25 bones — third-party tutorials documenting the UI), and an entire ecosystem of "add twist bones to a Mixamo rig" tutorials/fix-it threads exists because the export lacks them. Practitioner-consensus inference; Adobe never states it.
- Claim (medium): Cascadeur and Akeytsu are not weight solvers and should be excluded from an auto-skinning survey: Cascadeur's documented Quick Rigging Tool maps the *existing* joints of an already-skinned import onto its control-rig template (its config includes a "Twists and Untwists" section for recognizing twist joints — evidence twist handling is first-class in humanoid pipelines, but no skin-weight computation).
- Claim (low): AccuRIG's solver is a voxel/geodesic-style solve with per-joint falloff profiles plus template priors keyed to its guide points. Nothing public confirms this; it is speculation from behavior (tolerates multi-mesh, sloppy scans; per-limb quality claims; guide-point-driven placement) and from what the rest of the industry (Mixamo patent, Maya geodesic voxel) converged on.

## Technical detail

**Mixamo (Adobe; founders Corazza & Gambaretto) — documented via help pages + patents.**
Documented workflow: upload FBX/OBJ/ZIP → place markers (chin shown in UI; help text names wrists, elbows, knees, groin) → server rigs in minutes → optional Skeleton LOD (Standard 65 / fewer chains / No Fingers 25). Documented constraints: humanoid biped, near-symmetric neutral pose, connected geometry (no floating heads), no big appendages/props, nothing else in the file, origin-centered.
Patent US 8,797,328 B2 (the best public window into the actual method) adds: closed-form proxy construction; salient-point detection (automatic or manual — the markers); statistical skeleton fitting citing Pinocchio, including *deliberate insertion of intermediate joints "such as clavicles, mid-foot joints" because they are "useful in the skinning"*; finger counting by geodesic distance from the wrist; weight computation by segment-wise heat diffusion with per-joint learned diffusion coefficients/boundary conditions ("skinning templates ... specific for a given joint"); explicit per-region overrides ("local optimization parameters for specific areas such as the hands and the head where the profile of the skinning weight should be specific to the considered bone"); alternatives named: "diffusion, proximity maps and template matching"; transfer to the original mesh via mean value coordinates or NN + interpolation. Boundary note: a patent describes what was *claimed*, not necessarily what shipped in production at Adobe scale — treat the pipeline as "credibly inferred to be close to shipped," the per-joint-learned-templates idea as documented intent. A separate family (US 2020/0334892 A1, priority 2011) applies the same architecture (representative mesh → segments → key points → bone set → skinning weights) to facial rigging.
US 9,305,387 B2 documents the opposite regime for their character generator: PCA morphable body + artist-authored rigged templates in strict polygon correspondence → weights copied, skeleton re-fitted by MVC, artifacts cleaned with blendshape smoothing. Strong precedent for "retarget weights from a pre-weighted canonical body" — but documented only for the *generator*, not for arbitrary uploads (where the patent lists template matching merely as an option).

**Reallusion AccuRIG / Character Creator — documented workflow, undocumented solver.**
Documented: 5-step flow (Load → Check Model → Rig Body → Rig Hand → Calibrate → Add Motions); joints pre-placed "by approximations" then user-adjusted against a pictogram showing recommended joint locations (per-joint pick, mirror/symmetry, mask, midpoint placement, save/load joint statuses); hand step centers on knuckle positions and thumb orientation, finger count configurable; Calibrate step plays test motions and lets you rotate bones (e.g., shoulder) to fix penetration — i.e., post-bind joint-angle calibration, not weight editing. Free AccuRIG has *no* weight painting (CGDive confirms); Advanced AccuRIG inside CC adds Skin Weight Refinement, Auto Rig Selected Mesh, hard-surface auto-attach, skeleton layout editing, and re-rigging from an existing skeleton. Multi-mesh: meshes are classified deformable-vs-accessory before weighting; accessories bind rigidly to a neighboring bone; CC docs also describe adjusting neck joints "to avoid accessories and re-calculate skin weight." Claimed but mechanism-free: per-limb optimized weights (shoulder/elbow/knee/fingers), volume preservation for head/body/accessories, A/T/scan-pose tolerance. Inferred: twist bones in output (forum: "cc twist" bones appear even on UE export). Everything about the solver itself — energy, falloff, whether a canonical weighted template is fitted — is a black box; no patents found under Reallusion's name (assignee search returned unrelated hits; absence is suggestive, not proof).

**Maya (baseline "documented per-joint controls" in a DCC).**
Bind methods above; skinning modes Classic Linear / Dual Quaternion / Weight Blended (per-vertex DQ↔LBS blend map); Max Influences; Dropoff/Falloff rate (settable per influence via the skinCluster/Add Influence interface — command-reference level); Interactive Bind creates per-joint capsule or cylinder volumes seeded either from mesh volume or from a minimum-weight threshold, i.e., a documented per-joint editable falloff primitive. Heat Map's documented failure on dirty production meshes (Autodesk's wording) is the same failure mode Mixamo's proxy step and Maya's own voxelization exist to bypass — a consistent cross-vendor lesson: robustness comes from solving on a clean volumetric surrogate, not on the artist's mesh.

**What "shoulder-aware" plausibly means — labeled synthesis.**
(a) Per-joint falloff/diffusion profiles, learned from hand-skinned examples — documented (Mixamo patent); claimed without mechanism (AccuRIG); user-set (Maya dropoff). (b) Region/segment classification before solving — documented (Mixamo patents: segments as heat emitters; face patent's explicit "segments"); documented at mesh granularity (AccuRIG deformable-vs-hard-surface); inferred at body-part granularity for AccuRIG. (c) Template weight transfer from a canonical pre-weighted body — documented for Mixamo's generator (US 9,305,387); documented-as-option for uploads ("template matching"); speculative for AccuRIG. (d) Automatic twist-joint insertion — inferred for AccuRIG (forum evidence, CC skeleton); inferred-absent for Mixamo. (e) Intermediate-joint insertion for skinning quality (clavicle, mid-foot) — documented (Mixamo patent text). (f) Statistical joint placement from salient points + geodesic finger detection — documented (patent). (g) Pose-space deformation / corrective shapes added automatically on top of weights — no evidence in any of these tools' public material; speculation only (CC's hand-authored base bodies have correctives, but that is authored content, not auto-rigging output). (h) Volume preservation via rigid-locking heads/hard parts or DQS — claimed outcome (AccuRIG), mechanism speculative.

## What I'm unsure about

- Whether Adobe's *production* Mixamo service still runs the patented heat-diffusion + learned per-joint templates pipeline, or replaced it (e.g., with a learned regressor à la later Adobe research such as Neural Blend Shapes / RigNet-adjacent work). The service is a black box; the patent shows 2010–2014 intent only.
- AccuRIG's actual solver: no primary source at all. My voxel/geodesic + per-joint-profile guess is speculation; a template-fit-then-transfer design would look identical from the outside. Testing exported weight matrices against known algorithms (bounded biharmonic, geodesic voxel, heat) on a benchmark mesh would discriminate — not done here.
- Exact current Mixamo marker count/UI (chin marker) — the logged-in rigger UI wasn't exercised; I relied on help text + third-party tutorials. Also unverified: any hard polygon cap (older community lore mentions caps; current docs are silent).
- Whether AccuRIG's free build always emits twist bones or only under CC/Unreal export profiles, and whether those twist bones carry solved weights or procedural (driven) rotation only.
- Reallusion patent absence: searched assignee "Reallusion" on Google Patents only; filings under individual inventors' names or Taiwanese filings could exist and were not exhaustively searched.
- Maya per-influence dropoff: dialog-level docs show a single bind-time rate; per-influence dropoff is exposed at the skinCluster/Add Influence command level — I did not re-verify the command reference page this session.
