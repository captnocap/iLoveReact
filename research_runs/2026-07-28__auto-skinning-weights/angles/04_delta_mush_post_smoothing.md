# Angle 04 — Delta Mush & Post-Smoothing

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): Delta Mush is a detail-preserving smoothing post-process, not a weighting method: precompute per-vertex "deltas" (rest position expressed in a local frame built on a Laplacian-smoothed copy of the rest mesh), then at runtime smooth the crudely-deformed mesh the same way, rebuild the frames, and re-add the deltas. The R&H slides define it exactly: delta = "tangent space displacement vector", mush = smoothing, with `d_i = r_i · S_i^{-1}` at bind and `x'_i = d_i · S'_i` at runtime. Published as Mancewicz, Derksen, Rijpkema, Wilson, "Delta Mush: smoothing deformations while preserving detail," DigiPro 2014 (the four-author list incl. Rijpkema is on the DigiPro/ACM record; the parallel SIGGRAPH 2014 Talk lists only Mancewicz/Derksen/Wilson).
- Claim (high): The paper's own abstract states it was used "in all character rigs at Rhythm & Hues" since 2010, "simplifying rigging workflows while simultaneously improving rig performance," and the stated motivation is "fewer riggers, quicker rigs… eliminate fix shapes and PSD." The demonstrated production setup is literally crude binding rescued by smoothing: a screenshot captioned "proximity binding to skeleton with delta mush smoothing" with Mush Level 14, and the DDM paper later formalizes the same pipeline as "Delta Mush deformer on top of Rigid Bind Skinning" with 0/1 weights. This is direct primary evidence that rigid-ish weights + delta mush is a legitimate production strategy, not a hack.
- Claim (high): Classic DM cost is p Laplacian iterations per frame plus frame rebuild. R&H's numbers: 40,000-point mesh, 15 iterations = 4% of a 24 fps frame (~1.7 ms) on a Quadro K5200 (2014 GPU); their GPU port was ~75× the prototype (table: quad-core CPU 12×, GTX 680 63×, K5200 154×). Typical iteration counts in shipping tools: Maya's `deltaMush` node defaults to `smoothingIterations = 10`, `smoothingStep = 0.5`; Unreal's Deformer Graph demo hardcodes 8 uniform-Laplacian iterations; R&H used 14–15.
- Claim (high): Direct Delta Mush (Le & Lewis, "Direct delta mush skinning and variants," ACM TOG 38(4) Art. 113, SIGGRAPH 2019, EA SEED) removes runtime iteration entirely. Because Laplacian smoothing is linear, the smoothed position is a fixed weighted sum `A = (I − L)^p` over the rest mesh; solving a per-vertex weighted Procrustes problem against the LBS-deformed patch lets all pose-independent terms be precomputed into per-(vertex, bone) 4×4 symmetric "multi-weight" matrices `Ψ_ij = Σ_k A_ki w_kj u_k u_kᵀ` (10 floats each, sparse like scalar weights). Runtime per vertex: blend `Σ_j M_j Ψ_ij`, one 3×3 SVD → rotation, translation from the patch centers of rotation. One pass, GPU-friendly, no iteration/synchronization.
- Claim (high): DDM's variants v0–v5 form a documented quality/cost ladder, and several are exactly prior methods: v0 = full 4×4 weights + SVD (best; correctly turns pure joint translations into local rotation); v1 = same weights but inverse-transpose instead of SVD, which the authors state "produces exactly the same result as the original Delta Mush" (two-tangent frame) — it distorts under joint translation and can invert local frames in extreme poses; v2/v3 = one scalar rotation weight with quaternion (v2) or linear (v3) blending; v4 = two weights, equivalent to Skinning with Optimized Centers of Rotation (Le & Hodgins 2016); v5 = one scalar weight = plain LBS, "the same as baking original Delta Mush to Linear Blend Skinning using skinning decomposition" (candy-wrapper collapse returns at v5).
- Claim (high): Follow-ups exist and are primary-sourced: "Direct delta mush skinning compression with continuous examples" (Le, Villeneuve, Gonzalez-Ochoa, SIGGRAPH 2021) attacks DDM's cost (matrix weights + one 3×3 SVD per vertex) by splitting into a small DDM layer driving virtual bones + an LBS layer, minimizing error over a continuous (uncountable) pose set, reduced to linear matrix factorization. "Enhanced Direct Delta Mush" (Kalentchouk, Hutchinson, Tolani, SIGGRAPH Asia 2020 poster / arXiv:2101.02798) adds correct handling of non-rigid joint transforms, which stock DDM gets wrong.
- Claim (high, corrects the brief): Unreal's Deformer Graph sample (`DG_DeltaMushDeformerDemo.uasset` in Content Examples) is classic iterative Delta Mush, not DDM — Rodolphe Vaillant's breakdown documents 8 hardcoded uniform-smoothing iterations in the setup graph, deltas computed (suboptimally) in the update graph, and explicitly recommends DDM "in case speed is an issue." I found no evidence Epic ships a DDM implementation; treat "Unreal ships DDM" as unconfirmed.
- Claim (medium): Baking DM into cheap runtime weights is itself an established pipeline: the DDM talk cites the industry practice of fitting LBS weights to example poses generated by Delta Mush (Hans Godard's Skinning Converter, 2015) as "the main inspiration of our work." So even before DDM, studios used DM as the ground-truth generator and shipped the fitted LBS — a second flavor of "crude weights rescued after the fact."
- Claim (medium): Post-smoothing the weight field itself (iterated neighbor-averaging of weights, renormalized) is the standard cheap rescue: Maya ships it as Skin > Edit Smooth Skin > Smooth Skin Weights plus a paint-brush smooth operation that "uses the weights of neighbouring vertices"; third-party tools extend it. It bakes to zero runtime cost, but has no detail-preservation term — unlike DM it blurs the deformation rather than reconstructing detail.
- Claim (medium): Post-smoothing cannot fix wrong-bone assignment — averaging over neighbors only redistributes locally present influences, so a patch bound to the wrong bone stays wrong (the transition just gets softer); fixing it needs re-binding (e.g., geodesic voxel binding, Dionne & de Lasa SCA 2013, built precisely because Euclidean-proximity binding assigns weights to geometrically close but geodesically distant parts). Smoothing that gathers neighbors Euclidean-spatially (or volumetrically) re-introduces exactly that leakage across close-but-far surfaces (finger-to-finger, arm-to-torso); topology-based smoothing avoids jumping gaps but still bleeds through connected paths (palm webbing) as iterations grow. Direct primary sources spelling out this failure taxonomy are thin — it is practitioner consensus plus the documented motivation of geodesic binding — hence medium.
- Claim (high): DM/DDM's own limits are stated by the authors: DDM recap slide — "Limitations: no collision, no volume preservation, no secondary effect." DM needs mesh connectivity (Laplacian per connected component), so floating shells/accessories require the proximity/wrap binding R&H paired with it. Implicit skinning (Vaillant et al., "Implicit skinning: real-time skin deformation with contact modeling," SIGGRAPH 2013; elastic follow-up SIGGRAPH Asia 2014) is the contrast approach: also a post-process over LBS/DQS, but instead of smoothing, it re-projects vertices onto composed HRBF implicit surfaces per bone — heavier, but it delivers the self-contact and bulge effects DM explicitly does not.

## Technical detail

**Classic DM smoothing operator (from R&H slides).** Uniform: `x'_i = (1/N) Σ_j x'_j` over topological neighbors (optionally include self, 1/(N+1)). "Weight By Distance" variant: `x'_i = (Σ_j α_ij x'_j)/(1 + Σ_j α_ij)`, `α_ij = 1/||x'_i − x'_j||` — inverse-distance weighting "limits redistribution of points" (prevents vertex drift on irregular tessellation). Maya's node generalizes with `smoothingStep` s: `x ← x + s·(avg − x)`, default s = 0.5, 10 iterations; "Pin Border Vertices" = boundary condition (R&H "Pin Perimeter/Pin Convex").

**Delta storage.** Smooth the REST mesh p iterations once. At each vertex build frame `S_i` from the smoothed mesh (normal + an edge projected to the tangent plane, Gram-Schmidt — the UE sample does exactly this); store `d_i = r_i S_i^{-1}` (3 floats). Runtime: crude-skin → smooth p iterations → rebuild `S'_i` → `x'_i = d_i S'_i`. Any deformation upstream works (bones, blendshapes, wraps); the smooth kills crease/candy-wrapper/bulge artifacts, the delta restores modeled detail. Iterative smoothing needs p sequential dependent passes (the sync cost Le & Lewis cite as why DM resisted game-engine adoption).

**DDM precompute.** Per vertex i solve `min E(Γ_i) = Σ_k A_ki ||Γ_i u_k − v_k||²` with `A = (I − L)^p` (Laplacian matrix L, p = iteration count) — i.e., find the rigid transform of the red "local patch" that DM implies. Substitute LBS `v_k = Σ_j w_kj M_j u_k` (0/1 rigid weights suffice), expand the norm via traces, swap the vertex/bone sums: everything pose-independent collapses to `Ψ_ij = Σ_k A_ki w_kj u_k u_kᵀ` (homogeneous u ⇒ 4×4, symmetric ⇒ 10 floats, sparsity mirrors scalar weights). Implementation note from the talk: never form `(I − L)^p` explicitly — iterate Laplacian smoothing p times directly on the Ψ matrices.

**DDM runtime (v0).** `[Q_i q_i; p_iᵀ 1] = Σ_j M_j Ψ_ij`; SVD `U S Vᵀ = Q_i − q_i p_iᵀ`; `R_i = U Vᵀ`; `t_i = q_i − R_i p_i`. `p_i`/`q_i` are the rest/posed centers of rotation of the Laplacian patch. Extension: blending `[Q_i q_i; …]` with `[q_i p_iᵀ q_i; …]` changes rotation smoothing independently of translation smoothing, redistributable back into Ψ_ij at zero runtime cost — yields skin sliding, rigid-bend + smooth-twist, and DM's characteristic "negative bulging" (bend centerline stays inside the joint angle; LBS/DQS/CoR bulge outside).

**Variant ladder (storage per vertex-bone pair / rotation op):** v0: 10 floats / SVD. v1: 10 floats / inverse-transpose (≡ classic DM exactly). v2: scalar rotation weight + translation row / quaternion blend. v3: same / linear blend. v4: 2 weights / quaternion (≡ CoR 2016, gives a closed-form per-vertex CoR). v5: 1 scalar / linear (≡ LBS = DM baked to weights). Quality strictly decreases, performance increases; from v2 down, bone translations can no longer induce local rotation.

**Weight-field smoothing (contrast).** Operates on weights not positions: `w_i ← normalize(Σ_{j∈N(i)} w_j / |N(i)|)` per influence, iterated or brushed. Zero runtime cost (bakes into the bind), no extra data; but no delta/detail term, cannot create effects (sliding, negative bulge), and only softens transitions between influences already present locally.

## What I'm unsure about

- The exact ACM DOI landing content for the DigiPro paper (403 to WebFetch), so the "low-pass filter" phrasing that search summaries attribute to the abstract is second-hand; the R&H slides and SIGGRAPH History abstract are what I verified directly.
- DDM 2019 paper-internal benchmark tables (ms/vertex per variant, exact storage totals per model): the 21 MB paper PDF wasn't parsed page-by-page; my variant cost/precision statements come from the authors' own presentation with speaker notes, which ranks but doesn't fully quantify.
- Whether Epic ships any *Direct* Delta Mush anywhere (Deformer Graph plugin, experimental branches): unconfirmed; only the classic-DM sample is documented. Explicitly flagged as a correction to the brief's premise.
- Godard's Skinning Converter (2015) is cited from the DDM slides; it was a commercial/closed tool and I found no first-party technical writeup — the DM→LBS-fitting practice is documented mainly through Le & Lewis's citation of it. Proprietary, largely undocumented.
- The precise failure taxonomy of weight smoothing (wrong-bone assignment, palm bleed-through) is mechanism-derived plus practitioner consensus; I did not find a paper that studies weight-field post-smoothing failure modes head-on. If a stronger source is needed, Dionne & de Lasa's motivation section is the closest primary evidence.
- Maya's internal smooth-weight implementation (kernel, normalization order) is proprietary; the docs describe behavior, not the algorithm.
