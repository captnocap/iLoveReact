# Angle 06 — Hard Cases (Multi-Component, Broken, Low-Poly, Non-Human)

Method families referenced below: **HEAT** = surface bone-heat diffusion (Pinocchio; Blender "Automatic Weights"); **VOX** = volumetric geodesic voxel binding (Dionne & de Lasa; Maya "Geodesic Voxel"); **BBW** = bounded biharmonic weights and other volumetric-FEM optimizers; **NB** = nearest-bone / closest-point envelope assignment; **ML** = learned predictors (NeuroSkinning, RigNet, HeterSkinNet, Mixamo); **XFER** = weight transfer from a skinned proxy (Maya copySkinWeights; Abdrashitov et al. inpainting).

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): HEAT strands disconnected components by construction, and in Blender's implementation one bad island can kill the entire bind: all components share a single linear system, and if no vertex of some component is "visible" to any bone (floating teeth were the reported repro), the matrix is singular and the solve fails for every component — Blender lead dev Brecht Van Lommel's diagnosis on issue #45493, open since 2015 with multiple duplicates. [paper + bug-tracker]
- Claim (high): Pinocchio's paper states its assumptions outright: "The character mesh must be the boundary of a connected volume", given in roughly the skeleton's orientation/pose, "proportioned roughly like the given skeleton"; the heat system's visibility term H_jj = c/d(j)^2 is nonzero only if the vertex–bone segment lies inside the character volume, so a well-defined interior is load-bearing (removing that assumption is listed as future work). [paper]
- Claim (high): VOX is the family purpose-built for nasty input. The SCA 2013 paper targets meshes that are "non-manifold, non-watertight, have intersecting triangles, or comprise multiple connected components", and Maya's docs confirm multi-mesh selection is voxelized as a single volume giving "continuous weighting across the geometry" — i.e., it bridges components. Its own preconditions: all joints must lie inside the voxelized volume, front-face normals must point outward, and quality depends on voxel resolution. [paper + official docs]
- Claim (high): BBW's blocker on broken input is the tetrahedralization step, not the optimization: the volumetric discretization "brings with it meshing artifacts, the possibility of tetrahedralization failure, and the impossibility of generating weights for surfaces that are not closed" (Dodik et al. 2024, who fix this with a mesh-free Lagrangian formulation that runs on open surfaces and triangle soups). TetWild (Hu et al. 2018) and fTetWild (Hu et al., TOG 2020) largely removed the tet-failure mode — fTetWild meshes 98.7% of a 10k-model wild dataset in under 2 minutes including self-intersecting soups — but interior definition for open/layered shells remains the practical catch. [papers]
- Claim (high): The dedicated multi-component/garment solution is "Robust Skin Weights Transfer via Weight Inpainting" — Abdrashitov, Raichstat, Monsen, Hill, SIGGRAPH Asia 2023 Technical Communications. Affiliation is **Epic Games on all four authors** (the "Roblox" attribution circulating in some summaries — and in this run's seed pointer — is wrong; verified on the author's project page and the preprint header). Method: copy weights only where the closest point on the source body passes confidence gates (distance ≤ D = 0.05 × target-bbox diagonal, normal deviation ≤ Θ = 35°, barycentric interpolation on the matched triangle); all remaining vertices are inpainted by minimizing a mixed Dirichlet + Laplacian energy (cotan L, lumped mass M; quadratic problem, per-bone Cholesky solves), with partition-of-unity constraints and clamp-then-renormalize for slightly negative values (≥ −0.03 observed); optional Laplacian smoothing pass on unmatched regions. An extra pass with flipped normals catches inner layers of multilayered clothing whose normals face the body. [paper, read in full]
- Claim (high): Even the inpainting paper does not fully solve case (a): "Our method assumes that all clothing parts are connected. However, it's often the case that clothing is modeled as a set of disconnected patterns (i.e., the T-shirt sleeve is disconnected from the torso) or has small parts like buttons. We leave dealing with more complex topologies to future work." A disconnected island with zero confident matches has nothing for the Laplacian to propagate from. [paper]
- Claim (high): NB / plain closest-point never fails to produce weights on any input (it ignores connectivity entirely) but bleeds across logically distant bones. Pinocchio criticizes Maya-7-era proximity weighting for exactly this ("part of the torso may become attached to an arm"), and Abdrashitov et al. show the transfer-flavored version: sleeve vertices matched to the torso, a flared left pant leg picking up right-leg weights from a single vertex, dress bottoms snapping to the nearer leg. [papers]
- Claim (medium): For very low-poly meshes there is **no dedicated paper I could find**; the evidence is practitioner-side and thin. Game-dev practice for PS1-style/<1k-tri characters is rigid assignment — detach pieces and parent to bones, or flood vertices to 100% on one joint — rather than smooth auto weights (polycount threads exist but sit behind Cloudflare; content summarized only from search excerpts). Structural support: RigNet's own pipeline requires remeshing inputs to 1K–5K vertices, i.e., sub-1K meshes are outside its operating envelope. [practitioner, weakly sourced + repo README]
- Claim (medium): Proportion-agnosticism splits cleanly: HEAT/VOX/BBW weight computations contain no species or proportion prior (they consume only geometry + bone distances), so creatures/tails/multi-limb rigs are fine **given a skeleton already placed**; humanoid assumptions live in skeleton-fitting and learned stages. Evidence: Pinocchio's weights drove a donut, a hand, and a cat on hind legs with a biped skeleton, while its *embedding* failed exactly on proportion outliers (3 of 16 test bipeds needed a joint hint; extreme limb-size mismatch called out as the cause) and on thin limbs (bone radius below 2τ disconnects its discretization graph); Mixamo's FAQ restricts the auto-rigger to bipedal humanoids and warns "extra limbs, wings, and tails... may not work", quadrupeds unsupported. [papers + official FAQ]
- Claim (medium): The learned family has explicit hard-case branches: NeuroSkinning (Liu et al., TOG 38(4) 2019, NetEase Fuxi) targets production game characters; HeterSkinNet (Pan et al., I3D 2021) is built for "arbitrary topologies and morphologies (e.g., out-of-body bones, disconnected mesh components)" via a heterogeneous vertex/bone graph and a "HollowDist" vertex–bone metric. But generalization is data-bounded: successor papers (RigAnything 2025, Anymate 2025) report RigNet-class models "trained on small-scale datasets with less than 3K objects... generalize poorly", rest-pose-only, ~2 min/object. [paper abstracts + secondary paper statements]
- Claim (medium): Ecosystem signal for which family survives dirty input: the standard community fix when Blender's HEAT bind fails is a *voxel* add-on ("Voxel Heat Diffuse Skinning", mesh-online), marketed on "never fails to produce a result (no more 'Bone Heat Weighting failed' error)"; Brecht's in-tracker workaround is split → bind per piece → rejoin. [practitioner/marketplace + bug-tracker]

## Technical detail

Case × family behavior (labels: P = paper-backed, D = official docs, F = practitioner/forum, I = my inference):

**(a) Multi-component (shells, hair cards, sockets, floating eyes/teeth)**
- HEAT: components with no bone contact get zero/undefined weights (P: surface diffusion needs a surface path); Blender-specific: one fully-occluded island ⇒ singular global matrix ⇒ whole bind errors (bug-tracker, dev-diagnosed). Field survey: 8/46 downloaded characters threw the error; "every character that failed was assembled from many disconnected pieces" (F).
- VOX: bridges — all selected meshes voxelized as one solid; nearby shells (eyes in head, cards near scalp, garment over body) fall into the same geodesic field and inherit continuous weights (D). Nested closed shells (eyeballs inside a head) are not explicitly documented; bridging there is inferred from the single-volume treatment (I).
- BBW: needs a defined interior; layered/open garments make "inside" winding-number-ambiguous even when fTetWild produces a valid tet mesh (I); Abdrashitov et al. judge tet-meshing non-watertight clothing "may not be feasible in practice" (P).
- NB: assigns everything, bleeds across components/limbs (P).
- ML: HeterSkinNet explicitly handles disconnected components + out-of-body bones (P, abstract); RigNet consumes a mesh graph plus volumetric geodesic vertex–bone features — behavior on floating parts not stated (unknown).
- XFER: the standby — skin a proxy body, copy to attachments via Maya Skin > Copy Skin Weights (closest-point) (D; long-running workflow, e.g. Second Life clothing threads, F). Fails on loose garments (P: Fig. 1 failures). Inpainting fixes loose garments + multilayer (flipped-normal pass) but assumes connected clothing; user-painted masks force regions (collars) into the inpainted set (P). Ports: official Python/libigl sample code (MIT), unofficial Maya plugin (yamahigashi), Godot port (V-Sekai), third-party Blender add-on (D/F).

**(b) Holes / non-manifold**
- HEAT: solver failure modes are duplicate/degenerate geometry and undefined interior for the visibility term (P + F). Practitioner fix checklist, in order: merge-by-distance 0.0001, degenerate dissolve, recalculate normals outside, apply transforms / normalize scale (~1.8 m) — extreme scale alone can break the solve (F).
- VOX: designed-for (P title claim: non-manifold, non-watertight, intersecting, multi-component). Preconditions remain: joints enclosed by the volume, outward normals (D) — a hidden failure mode when helper bones stick out of the mesh (F).
- BBW: pre-2018 pipeline (TetGen-class CDT) fails on self-intersections/gaps and cannot do open surfaces (P); TetWild/fTetWild make tet meshes from soups routine (P: 98.7% < 2 min); alternatively Dodik et al. 2024 skip tets entirely and solve biharmonic energy mesh-free on soups/open surfaces (P).
- NB: indifferent to topology (I, trivially).
- ML: graph-based nets need usable connectivity for message passing; HeterSkinNet's pairwise vertex–bone design is the stated mitigation (P, abstract).

**(c) Very low-poly (<1000 tris)**
- All smooth methods: falloff band at a joint spans 1–2 edge rings, so "smooth" weights quantize to nearly rigid steps regardless of solver; cotan Laplacians additionally misbehave on the long/skinny/degenerate triangles typical of hand-optimized low-poly (I — mechanically sound but not paper-verified for skinning specifically).
- HEAT: works when geometry is clean but adds little over rigid assignment at this density (I); degenerate tris are a documented failure trigger via the cleanup checklist (F).
- VOX: weight field is computed volumetrically at chosen voxel resolution then sampled at vertices, so it degrades more gracefully than surface diffusion on bad tessellation (I from the paper's decoupled design); final smoothness still capped by vertex count (I).
- Practice: rigid 100%-per-joint or piece-parenting is the norm for PS1-style characters (F — polycount thread titles verified, bodies unreachable behind Cloudflare; treat as weakly sourced). One widely-linked Adobe forum complaint about Mixamo "smoothing" low-poly characters turned out to be viewport preview shading, not skinning — checked and discarded as evidence.
- ML: RigNet requires 1K–5K-vertex remeshes (D, repo README) ⇒ sub-1K inputs are out-of-distribution.

**(d) Non-human proportions (creatures, long limbs, tails, multi-limb)**
- HEAT/VOX/BBW weight solves: proportion-agnostic (P formulations contain only distances/volumes; Pinocchio demonstrated quadruped + centaur skeletons and a rigged donut).
- Skeleton-fitting stages: Pinocchio's embedding penalizes deviation from the given skeleton's proportions ("must be proportioned roughly like the given skeleton"); extreme limb-proportion outliers were its 3/16 failures; thin limbs below 2τ radius disconnect the embedding graph — direct hazard for long skinny tails/limbs (P).
- ML: Mixamo — bipedal humanoids only; wings/tails/extra limbs "may not work"; quadrupeds unsupported; internals proprietary and undocumented (D, Adobe FAQ). RigNet — template-free in architecture but data-bounded (~2,703 ModelsResource models; rest pose; successors report poor category generalization) (P + secondary). NeuroSkinning/HeterSkinNet — trained on game-production character distributions; cross-species behavior not documented (unknown).

## Evidence

- "The character mesh must be the boundary of a connected volume... The character must be given in approximately the same orientation and pose as the skeleton. Lastly, the character must be proportioned roughly like the given skeleton"; H_jj = c/d(j)^2 gated on the segment lying inside the volume; 13/16 embedding success, thin-limb 2τ failure, future work "eliminate the assumption that the character must have a well-defined interior" — read directly from the paper PDF — [Baran & Popović 2007, Automatic Rigging and Animation of 3D Characters (Pinocchio)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ibaran/papers/2007-SIGGRAPH-Pinocchio.pdf)
- Brecht Van Lommel: "They are all handled in a single matrix... if none of the vertices in a connected component are visible to any bones, then the matrix will not be invertible, and the solve will fail for all components" (teeth repro; split/bind/join workaround; still Open) — [Blender issue #45493](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/issues/45493)
- GVB targets meshes that are "non-manifold, non-watertight, have intersecting triangles, or comprise multiple connected components" — [Dionne & de Lasa 2013, Geodesic Voxel Binding for Production Character Meshes, SCA](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2485895.2485919)
- Maya: multi-mesh Geodesic Voxel bind "gives continuous weighting across the geometry since all shapes are treated as a single volume"; joints must be enclosed; normals outward — [Autodesk Maya docs, Geodesic Voxel binding](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/Maya-CharacterAnimation/files/GUID-5EFDB81B-E332-4D6C-B1BB-0B989AD2F2C7.htm)
- Full method + limits of weight inpainting (D = 0.05·d_box, Θ = 35°, Dirichlet+Laplacian QP, flipped-normal multilayer pass, "assumes that all clothing parts are connected", all-Epic author block) — read directly from the preprint PDF — [Abdrashitov et al. 2023, Robust Skin Weights Transfer via Weight Inpainting](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~rinat/projects/RobustSkinWeightsTransfer/preprint.pdf) ([project page](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~rinat/projects/RobustSkinWeightsTransfer/index.html), [code, MIT](https://github.com/rin-23/RobustSkinWeightsTransferCode))
- BBW's tet step "brings with it meshing artifacts, the possibility of tetrahedralization failure, and the impossibility of generating weights for surfaces that are not closed" — [Dodik, Sitzmann, Solomon, Stein 2024, Robust Biharmonic Skinning Using Geometric Fields, arXiv:2406.00238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00238)
- fTetWild: robust tet meshing of soups with self-intersections; 98.7% of inputs < 2 min — [Hu, Schneider, Wang, Zorin, Panozzo 2020, Fast Tetrahedral Meshing in the Wild, ACM TOG 39(4)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3386569.3392385) ([code](https://github.com/wildmeshing/fTetWild))
- Practitioner failure survey: 46 characters, 8 threw the bone-heat error, "every character that failed was assembled from many disconnected pieces"; ordered fix checklist (non-manifold cleanup → per-piece binding → bone placement → scale/transforms) — [Jessy Leite, Fix "Bone Heat Weighting failed" in Blender](https://jessyleite.dev/posts/blender-bone-heat-weighting-failed/) (practitioner report)
- Proxy-transfer standby: official closest-point copy tool — [Autodesk Maya docs, Copy smooth skin weights](https://help.autodesk.com/view/MAYAUL/2024/ENU/?guid=GUID-7D895BB0-1522-4388-96E0-4245127F90AB); long-running garment workflow discussion — [Second Life forums: copy skin weights from mesh body to clothing](https://community.secondlife.com/forums/topic/404716-maya-copy-skin-weights-from-mesh-body-and-overlap-with-clothing/) (practitioner)
- HeterSkinNet handles "arbitrary topologies and morphologies (e.g., out-of-body bones, disconnected mesh components, etc.)" — [Pan et al. 2021, HeterSkinNet, I3D, arXiv:2103.10602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.10602)
- RigNet dataset/preprocessing: ModelsResource-RigNetv1, 2,703 models; inputs remeshed to 1K–5K vertices; volumetric geodesic vertex–bone features — [RigNet GitHub README](https://github.com/zhan-xu/RigNet); rest-pose-only + poor small-dataset generalization stated by successors — [RigAnything, arXiv:2502.09615](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09615v1), [Anymate, arXiv:2505.06227](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06227v1)
- Mixamo auto-rigger: humanoid bipeds only; "extra limbs, wings, and tails... may not work"; quadrupeds unsupported; algorithm undocumented/proprietary — [Adobe Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html) (official docs)
- Community voxel fallback marketed as "never fails" vs. bone heat — [mesh-online, Voxel Heat Diffuse Skinning](https://www.mesh-online.net/voxel.html), [Superhive listing](https://superhivemarket.com/products/voxel-heat-diffuse-skinning) (practitioner/marketplace)
- NeuroSkinning venue/authors (NetEase Fuxi) — [Liu et al. 2019, NeuroSkinning, ACM TOG 38(4)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3306346.3322969)

## What I'm unsure about

- **GVB on fully nested shells** (eyeballs sealed inside a closed head): Maya's docs guarantee continuous weighting across multi-mesh volumes, but I found no explicit statement or test for interior-floating closed shells; "bridges via shared volume" there is my inference.
- **fTetWild + BBW on garments/multi-component characters**: I found no published pipeline actually doing this; my claim that winding-number interior ambiguity is the sticking point is inference, and the strongest counter-signal (Abdrashitov calling it infeasible) predates any such attempt in production.
- **Low-poly evidence is the weakest of the four cases**: polycount thread bodies were unreachable (Cloudflare interstitial, even via a real browser session), so the rigid-bind-for-low-poly norm rests on search excerpts and general practice, not directly quoted posts. No paper evaluates auto-skinning below ~1K vertices as far as I found.
- **RigNet input orientation**: rest-pose requirement is verified via successor papers; a commonly repeated upright/front-facing orientation requirement I could not verify in the README extract — left out of claims.
- **ModelsResource-RigNetv1 category mix** (humanoid vs. quadruped vs. other proportions): dataset size verified; per-category composition not verified.
- **Mixamo internals** are proprietary and undocumented — only the input restrictions in Adobe's FAQ are citable; anything about *how* it skins would be speculation, so I make no such claim.
- The Blender projects.blender.org page rendered through a browser session; comment timestamps displayed oddly (several early comments show identical times), so I treat the thread's who-said-what as reliable but exact dates as approximate.
