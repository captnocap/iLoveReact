# Angle 02 — Bounded Biharmonic Weights

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): The exact formulation (paper Eq. 2–6) is: minimize Σ_j ½ ∫_Ω ||Δw_j||² dV over all m handle weight functions jointly, subject to (3) interpolation w_j|H_k = δ_jk on handles, (4) w_j linear on every cage face, (5) partition of unity Σ_j w_j(p) = 1 ∀p ∈ Ω, and (6) bounds 0 ≤ w_j(p) ≤ 1. Without the inequality constraints the Euler–Lagrange equations are the biharmonic PDE Δ²w_j = 0, hence the name; with them, BBW is a constrained QP, not a PDE solve. Verified against the paper PDF. — Jacobson, Baran, Popović, Sorkine-Hornung, "Bounded Biharmonic Weights for Real-Time Deformation," ACM SIGGRAPH 2011 (also CACM Research Highlight, Apr 2014).
- Claim (high): The energy is integrated over the VOLUME Ω enclosed by the shape, not over the surface. This is what makes weights "shape-aware": the bilaplacian is determined by the metric of Ω alone, so influence follows the interior of the solid (around concavities) rather than Euclidean distance. Consequence: 3D requires a tetrahedral mesh of the interior; 2D requires a triangulation of the region. The FEM mesh must conform to the handles — the paper samples vertices along skeleton bones and on cage faces and meshes Ω "in a way compatible with all of the handles and the vertices of S."
- Claim (high): Discretization is linear FEM using the "flattened" mixed-FEM treatment of the fourth-order problem (Jacobson et al., "Mixed Finite Elements for Variational Surface Modeling," SGP 2010): with lumped mass matrix M and cotangent stiffness L, Σ_j ½∫||Δw_j||² dV ≈ ½ Σ_j w_jᵀ (L M⁻¹ L) w_j (paper Eq. 8). libigl builds exactly this quadratic form via `igl::harmonic(V,Ele,2,Q)` inside `igl::bbw`.
- Claim (high): The paper's solver is MOSEK (sparse convex QP), solving all handles simultaneously; but because QP time is superlinear in unknowns, the authors' recommended fast path DROPS the partition-of-unity constraint, solves each handle independently, and normalizes per-vertex afterward. Reported: 50× faster on the 7-handle Gargoyle, "mostly negligible" differences (Table 2: E_mean ≈ 2e-3–5e-3, E_max up to 0.4 far from handles), "visually indistinguishable" deformations (Fig. 6). Every practical implementation I found (libigl, the MATLAB demo) uses the per-handle + normalize path.
- Claim (high): libigl's `igl::bbw(V,Ele,b,bc,data,W)` implements only the per-handle path: `bbw.cpp` contains `assert(!data.partition_unity && "partition_unity not implemented yet")`, loops over handles with `parallel_for(m,optimize_weight,2)`, and calls `igl::active_set` per weight column with bounds lx=0, ux=1. The official tutorial (chapter 403) then normalizes explicitly: `W = (W.array().colwise() / W.array().rowwise().sum()).eval();`. MOSEK remains available as an alternative QP backend via a separate header `include/igl/mosek/bbw.h`.
- Claim (high): Bind-time cost on 2011 hardware (Mac Pro quad Xeon 2.66 GHz, "unoptimized code", MOSEK), from Table 2, bind time PER HANDLE in seconds: Gingerman (5,040 elements) 0.14 s; Mona Lisa (32,258) 1.24 s; Gargoyle (46,003 elements, 20k input tris) 3.19 s; Hand (51,263) 3.33 s; Mouse (112,355) 8.45 s; Armadillo (142,073 elements, 86,442 input tris) 12.09 s. So a 20-bone character on a ~140k-element tet mesh was minutes of total bind time in 2011; per-handle solves parallelize trivially.
- Claim (high): Mesh sizes are modest because TetGen produced GRADED tet meshes (large interior tets, fine near the surface/handles): the 43,234-vertex Armadillo surface plus 120 vertices sampled along bones yielded a 46,898-vertex tet mesh; TetGen "takes a few seconds" for all their 3D examples. 2D used Shewchuk's Triangle with near-uniform, pixel-scale triangles (< 1 s).
- Claim (high): Quality properties, per the paper's own Table 1: smoothness (C¹ at handles, C∞ elsewhere), non-negativity, shape-awareness, and partition of unity hold by construction; locality/sparsity and "no local maxima" are marked Y* — "only experimental confirmation," no formal proof. The bound constraints are what empirically suppress the spurious local extrema that unconstrained biharmonic weights exhibit (Figs. 4, 16).
- Claim (high): Known drawbacks stated by the authors: (a) bind-time optimization cost; (b) volumetric discretization required in 3D; (c) NO linear precision — BBW does not reproduce linear functions, so it cannot be used as pure cage coordinates that only interpolate cage-vertex positions without distortion under cage rotation (BBW instead blends full affine transforms per handle; Fig. 17 shows the locality-vs-linear-precision trade-off against Harmonic Coordinates); (d) deformation is still LBS, so the mapping can fold over / self-intersect (non-injective); (e) they only tested linear blending, though the weights can feed dual-quaternion skinning.
- Claim (high): Handle-type behavior: point handles give C¹ weights at the handle; BONES get w=1 along the entire segment, forcing a weight discontinuity at shared joints (w_j=1 on bone j must be 0 on the adjacent bone) — harmless for deformation because all bones sharing a joint transform it to the same location, and the paper splits weights equally among bones at shared joint vertices; CAGES get explicit linear-interpolation constraints on cage faces (Eq. 4), which makes weights non-smooth at cage VERTICES (smooth everywhere else, including across cage faces), and partial/open cages are supported — cage faces are not required to enclose the object.
- Claim (high): A practical gift for implementers: "once a volume is computed, an arbitrary embedded object (e.g., polygon soup) may be deformed without regard for its topology" — the render mesh need not be manifold or watertight for the SOLVE; the tet mesh is the computation domain and weights transfer to any embedded points (barycentric coordinates in the containing tet). The tetrahedralizer's input-quality demands are the real constraint.
- Claim (high): TetGen is the fragility point: its input must be a valid PLC (facets closed under intersection, no self-intersections); on detecting a self-intersection TetGen "stops," and the manual concedes models from most CAD tools do not satisfy the PLC condition (the `-d` switch exists just to report intersecting facet pairs). Modern replacements: TetWild ("Tetrahedral Meshing in the Wild," Hu, Zhou, Gao, Jacobson, Zorin, Panozzo, SIGGRAPH 2018) is unconditionally robust, converting raw triangle soup to an analysis-ready tet mesh via filtered exact construction + an explicit ε-envelope tolerance + validity-preserving mesh improvement; fTetWild ("Fast Tetrahedral Meshing in the Wild," Hu, Schneider, Wang, Zorin, Panozzo, SIGGRAPH 2020) keeps the robustness guarantee, always maintains a valid floating-point mesh, and runs at speeds comparable to Delaunay-based tools.
- Claim (medium): TetWild/fTetWild remesh the boundary within the ε-envelope, so the tet mesh's surface is NOT vertex-identical to your render mesh — an implementation must embed the original vertices back into the tet mesh (point-in-tet + barycentric weights) rather than assume index correspondence; and neither tool documents a first-class way to make the tet mesh CONFORM to interior bone-sample points the way the paper's TetGen setup did (TetGen accepts extra input points), so bone constraints must be imposed by snapping to nearest tet vertices, local refinement, or constraining all vertices inside a small capsule around each bone. This gap is real but the workarounds are folklore, not documented primary-source procedure.
- Claim (medium): libigl's active-set solver is a pragmatic, not certified, QP solver for this problem: the official tutorial caps it at `bbw_data.active_set_params.max_iter = 8` and the solver reports CONVERGED / MAX_ITER / ERROR statuses; there is no published benchmark of its solve time or optimality gap vs MOSEK. Treat "8 iterations, then normalize" as the community-standard recipe with quality accepted visually, not numerically.
- Claim (high): Monotonicity follow-up: "Smooth Shape-Aware Functions with Controlled Extrema" (Jacobson, Weinkauf, Sorkine-Hornung, SGP 2012) directly targets BBW's unproven extrema behavior — bounding higher-order energies "encourages oscillation" — by letting the user prescribe where extrema may occur and enforcing monotonicity via inequality-constrained quadratic optimization; reference code is `monotonic_biharmonic.m` in gptoolbox. Practical read: costlier solve, only worth it if you observe spurious bulges from stray weight extrema.
- Claim (high): "Fast Automatic Skinning Transformations" (Jacobson, Baran, Kavan, Popović, Sorkine-Hornung, SIGGRAPH 2012) is a POSE-time complement, not a weight method: given (typically BBW) weights, the user specifies only a subset of handle transforms and the rest are inferred by minimizing an as-rigid-as-possible energy restricted to the skinning subspace (~100 armadillos of 86k tris each animated at 30 fps on one CPU core). For an implementer it means BBW rigs can be driven from very sparse input.
- Claim (high): Attribution correction to the seed pointers: "Biharmonic Coordinates" (Computer Graphics Forum 2012) is by Weber, Poranne, Gotsman — a DIFFERENT group — and generalizes harmonic coordinates for cages (boundary + derivative interpolation); it is not a Jacobson-group BBW follow-up. The same-group cheap-alternative lineage is "Linear Subspace Design for Real-Time Shape Deformation" (Wang, Jacobson, Barbič, Kavan, SIGGRAPH 2015), which builds handle subspaces by solving only sparse LINEAR systems (no inequality-constrained QP), unifying LBS-style weights and generalized barycentric coordinates.
- Claim (low): For a custom tool, expected modern bind cost is seconds-per-handle at ~100–200k tets using libigl's active-set path (extrapolating Table 2 plus a decade of hardware); I found no authoritative modern benchmark, so budget for background/async bind-time computation rather than interactive re-solve.

## Technical detail

Continuous problem (per paper Eq. 2–6), Ω ⊂ R² or R³ the region enclosed by the shape, handles H_j (points, bone segments, cage vertices):

  min over {w_j}  Σ_j ½ ∫_Ω ||Δw_j||² dV
  s.t.  w_j|H_k = δ_jk;  w_j linear on each cage face;  Σ_j w_j ≡ 1;  0 ≤ w_j ≤ 1.

Discrete pipeline (what you actually implement):
1. Volumetric domain: 2D — constrained Delaunay triangulation (Triangle). 3D — tet mesh of the interior. Clean watertight input: TetGen with graded sizing (coarse interior, fine near surface). Arbitrary "wild" input: fTetWild with ε-envelope, then filter interior tets (winding number) and embed the render mesh by point-in-tet barycentric coordinates.
2. Conform to handles: add handle samples as mesh vertices — points directly; bones sampled along the segment (the paper used ~dozens of samples, e.g. 120 for the Armadillo skeleton); cage vertices as vertices, cage faces flagged for linearity constraints.
3. Matrices: cotangent stiffness L (2D cotan Laplacian / its 3D tet equivalent), lumped Voronoi mass M. Quadratic form A = L M⁻¹ L (sparse, PSD; same as `igl::harmonic(V,Ele,k=2,Q)`).
4. Constraints per handle j: fixed values w_j = 1 on H_j's vertices, w_j = 0 on all other handles' vertices (`igl::boundary_conditions(V,T,C,P,BE,CE,...,b,bc)` builds b/bc from a skeleton: point handles P, bone edges BE, cage edges CE); box bounds 0 ≤ w ≤ 1 everywhere; optional linear equality rows for cage faces; optional extra quadratic term for shape-preservation brushes: add ½∫_Π ρ||∇w_j||² ≈ wᵀ(GᵀR M̄ G)w (FEM gradient G; sparsity a subset of A, so no extra fill).
5. Solve m independent QPs: min ½wᵀAw s.t. fixed values + bounds. Options: (a) MOSEK / OSQP / any convex QP; (b) libigl `active_set`: iterate {guess active bound set → solve equality-constrained KKT system with `min_quad_with_fixed` (sparse factorization) → update active set}, capped at ~8 iterations. Warm-starting from the previous rig edit is natural but not provided out of the box.
6. Normalize: W(i,:) /= Σ_j W(i,j). (This replaces constraint (5); the paper measured E_mean ~5e-3 vs the joint solve.)
7. Runtime: plain LBS (`igl::lbs_matrix`, U = M_lbs · T_stack) or DQS on GPU; weights are static after bind.

Complexity: A has O(n) nonzeros (n = tet-mesh vertices); each active-set iteration is a sparse SPD-ish factorization/solve, so per-handle cost ≈ (few) × (Cholesky of an n-sized bilaplacian system); total scales linearly in handle count and superlinearly in n — the reason the joint (all-handles + partition-of-unity) QP of size n·m is avoided.

## What I'm unsure about

- No authoritative modern timing exists for the libigl active-set BBW path (Table 2 is 2011 hardware with MOSEK); my seconds-per-handle-at-100–200k-tets expectation is extrapolation, not measurement.
- Whether `active_set` at the tutorial's max_iter=8 reliably reaches the QP optimum, and its optimality gap vs MOSEK, is undocumented anywhere I could find — the community treats visual quality as the acceptance test.
- The precise recommended procedure for making a TetWild/fTetWild tet mesh conform to interior bone samples (the paper relied on TetGen accepting handle samples as input points) is not documented by either project; snapping/refinement workarounds are folklore. An implementer should verify against fTetWild's input options or plan a post-mesh constraint-vertex selection step.
- The paper's Table 2 "|Ω|" column is captioned "number of elements in the discretization" — I read it as triangle/tet counts (consistent with Armadillo: 142,073 elements vs the 46,898 tet-mesh vertices quoted in the text), but the caption doesn't say "tetrahedra" explicitly.
- I characterized "Linear Subspace Design" (2015) from its abstract and project materials only, not a full read; the exact constraint structure of its weights (affine precision, sign behavior) needs the paper itself before copying into an implementation.
- gptoolbox reportedly contains a `robust_bbw`-style MATLAB pipeline (mesh repair + tetrahedralize + bbw); I did not verify its existence or contents, so I have not cited it.
- Whether MOSEK's current license terms still make it viable for a shipping tool (it was academic-licensed in 2011-era workflows) — unverified here; OSQP or the libigl active-set path avoids the question.
