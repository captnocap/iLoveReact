# Angle 05 — Neural / Learned Skinning

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): The lineage is real and precisely citable: **NeuroSkinning: Automatic Skin Binding for Production Characters with Deep Graph Networks** (Lijuan Liu, Youyi Zheng, Di Tang, Yi Yuan, Changjie Fan, Kun Zhou — SIGGRAPH/ACM TOG 2019, NetEase Fuxi AI Lab); **RigNet: Neural Rigging for Articulated Characters** (Zhan Xu, Yang Zhou, Evangelos Kalogerakis, Chris Landreth, Karan Singh — SIGGRAPH 2020); **HeterSkinNet: A Heterogeneous Network for Skin Weights Prediction** (Xiaoyu Pan, Jiancong Huang, Jiaming Mai, He Wang, Honglin Li, Tongkui Su, Wenjun Wang, Xiaogang Jin — I3D 2021, PACMCGIT 4(1)); **Learning Skeletal Articulations with Neural Blend Shapes** (Peizhuo Li, Kfir Aberman, Rana Hanocka, Libin Liu, Olga Sorkine-Hornung, Baoquan Chen — SIGGRAPH 2021); **SkinningNet: Two-Stream Graph Convolutional Neural Network for Skinning Prediction of Synthetic Characters** (Albert Mosella-Montoro, Javier Ruiz-Hidalgo — CVPR 2022); **Skeleton-free Pose Transfer for Stylized 3D Characters** (Zhouyingcheng Liao, Jimei Yang, Jun Saito, Gerard Pons-Moll, Yang Zhou — ECCV 2022); **TARig: Adaptive template-aware neural rigging for humanoid characters** (Jing Ma, Dongliang Zhang — Computers & Graphics vol. 114, 2023).
- Claim (high): Both seeded 2025 names check out. **UniRig** ("One Model to Rig Them All: Diverse Skeleton Rigging with UniRig", arXiv:2504.12451, SIGGRAPH 2025 / ACM TOG, Tsinghua + VAST/Tripo, code MIT) and **MagicArticulate** ("MagicArticulate: Make Your 3D Models Articulation-Ready", arXiv:2502.12135, CVPR 2025, Seed3D/ByteDance, code Apache-2.0) exist with public repos and checkpoints. **Make-It-Animatable** (arXiv:2411.18197, CVPR 2025, MIT) also checks out, with a HuggingFace demo.
- Claim (high): **RigNet's code is dual-licensed "GPLv3 or Commercial License"** (commercial via UMass Amherst Technology Transfer Office, tto@umass.edu). Embedding it in a closed-source tool triggers GPLv3 unless you buy the commercial license. Its dataset **ModelsResource-RigNetv1** (2,703 rigged models; all meshes normalized to 1K–5K vertices) has no clearly stated per-model license; a third-party production adopter (V-Sekai) described the files as having "unclear licenses" and proposed rebuilding a CC-BY dataset instead. The models come from a fan-asset aggregation site (The Models Resource), so commercial training use is legally murky — treat as research-only.
- Claim (high): Architecturally the field moved in two waves: 2019–2022 = **graph convolutions over mesh(+skeleton) graphs regressing per-vertex weight vectors** (NeuroSkinning, RigNet's GMEdgeNet, HeterSkinNet's heterogeneous vertex/bone graph with its "HollowDist" vertex-bone distance, SkinningNet's two-stream mesh/skeleton GCN with multi-aggregator convolution); 2024–2026 = **autoregressive transformers generating skeletons as token sequences plus a separate skinning head** (UniRig: skeleton-tree tokenization + bone-point cross-attention; MagicArticulate: autoregressive skeleton + "functional diffusion" skinning with volumetric-geodesic priors; also RigAnything, arXiv:2502.09615, template-free autoregressive).
- Claim (high): **Inference cost is solved in the newest wave**: Make-It-Animatable's CVPR 2025 paper reports ~0.5 s per character for itself vs ~10 min for RigNet and ~0.6 min for TARig. Caveat: these are a competitor's measurements, but the ~10 min RigNet figure matches its multi-stage CPU-heavy pipeline (mean-shift clustering + geodesic features).
- Claim (high): **Input assumptions persist even in "general" systems.** RigNet requires remeshing to 1K–5K vertices; Neural Blend Shapes requires triangulated, consistently upright/front-facing, spatially aligned meshes and its pretrained model is "dedicated for biped characters"; Make-It-Animatable is explicitly humanoid ("any 3D humanoid model", trained on Mixamo + 3DBiCar), though it accepts arbitrary input pose and both meshes and 3D Gaussian splats; TARig is humanoid-template (21 template joints + adaptive secondary joints). None strictly requires watertight meshes — graph/point networks tolerate open meshes, and HeterSkinNet explicitly claims robustness to "out-of-body bones, disconnected mesh components".
- Claim (high): **Commercial services document the same restrictions the papers have.** Meshy: humanoid/biped in A- or T-pose rigs best, clean topology, keep under ~100K faces, retopologize AI-generated meshes first; exported rigs follow the Mixamo skeleton spec. Tripo: claims humans, animals, and stylized characters, recommends clean FBX/GLTF at realistic human scale (~1.8 units). Anything World: "proprietary Machine Learning algorithms can understand almost any 3D model, rig it and add animations" — no method documentation at all. All three methods are proprietary and undocumented; that they are neural is stated only by Anything World ("ML algorithms") and implied for Tripo (co-author of UniRig).
- Claim (medium): **Training-data scale was the binding constraint through 2023 and is the main axis of progress since.** Everything through TARig trained on ≤2.7K models (ModelsResource) or private studio data (NeuroSkinning: artist-painted production characters, dataset never publicly released; HeterSkinNet: production characters, no public code found). The new wave scaled to Rig-XL (14K+ rigged models, Objaverse-sourced + VRoid), Articulation-XL2.0 (48K, filtered from Objaverse-XL), and Anymate (230K assets, "70 times larger than existing datasets", SIGGRAPH 2025).
- Claim (medium): **No shipped DCC or engine publicly documents cross-character neural weight prediction in production.** Tripo, Meshy, and Anything World ship auto-rigging that is almost certainly learned, but none discloses its production model. Tripo co-developed UniRig and open-sourced it, yet its own blog post does not state that UniRig powers the shipped rig button ("not yet integrated into Tripo Studio" at the time of the post). Adobe Mixamo's long-standing auto-rigger is proprietary and undocumented (and per Make-It-Animatable's evaluation, requires near-T/A-pose and predicts no finger bones).
- Claim (high): **What has shipped in production engines is per-character learned deformation approximation, not weight prediction**: Unreal Engine 5's ML Deformer / Neural Morph Model (official Epic docs, including production guidance — train on Maya-simulated deformation data, runtime network outputs compressed morph-target weights) and ZivaRT (real-time learned tissue deformation; Unity discontinued all Ziva products April 2024, with an exclusive perpetual IP license going to DNEG). These approximate a gold-standard rig for one character; they do not invent weights for new characters, but they prove learned deformation passes production QA bars.
- Claim (medium): **Editability/determinism**: the weight-prediction family outputs plain joints + LBS weights, fully editable in any DCC — a real advantage over implicit/neural-deformer approaches (Neural Blend Shapes' corrective blend-shape branch and Skeleton-Free Pose Transfer's part-based deformation are less portable). RigNet even exposes interactive knobs (mean-shift `bandwidth` and density `threshold` in `quick_start.py` control joint density). Autoregressive skeleton generators (UniRig, MagicArticulate) sample token sequences, so run-to-run determinism depends on seeding/temperature settings — verify before promising reproducible rigs.
- Claim (medium): **Out-of-distribution failure is the dominant quality risk.** Make-It-Animatable's evaluations report: RigNet produces "unnecessary topological structures" and weights that "fail to maintain spatial smoothness"; Neural Blend Shapes "only supports T-pose inputs"; Meshy "tends to enlarge the influence regions of skinning weights" and "fails to extract the rest pose"; Tripo produces "inaccurate and unmatched bones" on hard inputs; Anything World "can only deal with simple input poses (T- or A-pose)". SMPL-template methods are "limited to shapes resembling realistic humans". Competitor-reported, but consistent with each tool's own documentation.
- Claim (low): **Checkpoint/dataset provenance is an unresolved legal question for commercial use.** UniRig and MagicArticulate code is MIT/Apache, but their released checkpoints were trained on Objaverse-XL-derived datasets whose per-model licenses vary; neither repo states license clearance per training asset. A developer shipping these weights commercially is assuming untested legal risk; training your own model on licensed/owned rigs (as NeuroSkinning did at NetEase) is the conservative route.
- Claim (medium): For an implementer today, the practical starting points are **UniRig (MIT, skeleton+skin, diverse categories) and Make-It-Animatable (MIT, humanoid, <1 s, arbitrary pose)**; RigNet remains the canonical benchmark but is GPL-encumbered, slow, and dataset-limited; classical geodesic/heat/bounded-biharmonic methods remain the deterministic fallback for a tool that must never fail weirdly.

## Technical detail

- **NeuroSkinning (2019)** — GCN over a graph built from mesh + skeleton hierarchy in rest pose; per-node mesh-skeleton attributes; learns from artist-painted production characters; a "super-skeleton" (sparse matrix) generalizes across differing skeletons. In: mesh + skeleton. Out: weight map. Data: internal NetEase (not released). Code: NOT released — the FuxiCV/NeuroSkinning repo holds only images + README, no license file.
- **RigNet (2020)** — multi-stage: GMEdgeNet regresses vertex displacements → mean-shift clustering → joints; BoneNet + MST for connectivity; GMEdgeNet skinning head using volumetric-geodesic vertex-bone features. In: mesh, 1K–5K verts, no skeleton required. Out: joints + hierarchy + weights. Data: ModelsResource-RigNetv1 (2,703). License: GPLv3 or commercial (UMass TTO).
- **HeterSkinNet (2021)** — heterogeneous graph (vertex nodes + bone nodes), custom conv transferring info between node types via "HollowDist"; handles out-of-body bones, disconnected components. In: mesh + given skeleton. Out: weights. Data: production characters + ModelsResource eval. No public code found.
- **Neural Blend Shapes (2021)** — MeshCNN + skeleton-aware operators; envelope branch predicts skeleton + skinning for a fixed biped template; residual branch adds pose-dependent corrective blend shapes. In: triangulated, aligned, upright biped mesh (indifferent to connectivity). Out: BVH skeleton + .npy weights + blend shapes. Data: SMPL + MultiGarmentNetwork + Mixamo. License: BSD 2-Clause (bundles MIT deps).
- **SkinningNet (2022)** — two-stream GCN (mesh graph + skeleton graph) joined by a mesh-skeleton graph; Multi-Aggregator Graph Convolution; end-to-end, no handcrafted vertex-bone features. In: mesh + skeleton. Out: weights. Code status uncertain: github.com/imatge-upc/skinningnet's default branch is the gh-pages website (MIT-labeled); a full training-code release could not be confirmed.
- **Skeleton-Free Pose Transfer (2022)** — predicts sparse per-part skinning weights + per-part transformations jointly to deform an unrigged, non-T-pose stylized character to a driver pose; semi-supervised on Mixamo/AMASS/RigNet data. License: **Adobe Research License — noncommercial research only** ("may be exercised for noncommercial research purposes... only").
- **TARig (2023)** — humanoid template (21 joints) + adaptively generated secondary joints for accessories; shared GNN backbone; "boneflow" field disambiguates secondary-joint connectivity; anatomic-label supervision. In: humanoid mesh. Out: template skeleton + weights. Published Computers & Graphics 114; no public code found.
- **Make-It-Animatable (2024→CVPR 2025)** — particle-based shape autoencoder; coarse-to-fine, structure-aware prediction of blend weights + bones + pose transforms in ~0.5 s; handles arbitrary input pose; meshes and 3D Gaussian splats; predicts fingers. Data: Mixamo (+3DBiCar samples for unusual head-body ratios). License: MIT; pretrained weights on HuggingFace.
- **UniRig (2025)** — GPT-style autoregressive transformer over a Skeleton Tree Tokenization → topologically valid skeleton; Bone-Point Cross-Attention head → per-vertex weights + bone attributes. In: .obj/.fbx/.glb/.vrm mesh. Out: skeleton + weights. Data: Rig-XL (14K+) + VRoid; released checkpoints were trained on Articulation-XL2.0, with Rig-XL/VRoid checkpoints listed as "planned". License: MIT (verified raw LICENSE). Claims 215% rigging-accuracy / 194% motion-accuracy improvement over SOTA.
- **MagicArticulate (2025)** — skeleton as sequence modeling (autoregressive transformer, handles variable bone counts); skinning via functional diffusion with volumetric geodesic distance priors. Data: Articulation-XL2.0 (48K, from Objaverse-XL, includes skinning matrices in NPZ). License: Apache-2.0; skeleton-generation inference code + weights released; standalone skinning-prediction code release not confirmed in README.
- **Ecosystem 2025–26**: Anymate (SIGGRAPH 2025; 230K-asset dataset + modular joint/connectivity/skinning baselines); HumanRig (large humanoid dataset, CVPR 2025-era — not deep-verified here); RigAnything (template-free autoregressive); Unreal ML Deformer / Neural Morph Model (shipped, per-character deformation approximation, CPU inference emitting morph-target weights).

## What I'm unsure about

- **NeuroSkinning's training-set size and whether it actually shipped inside NetEase's production pipeline** — the paper targets "production characters" and came from NetEase Fuxi, but I found no public post-2019 statement confirming deployment; dataset/code were never released.
- **SkinningNet code**: the repo's visible default branch is the gh-pages website; I could not confirm the training/inference code was ever published. HeterSkinNet and TARig: no public code found — absence not proven.
- **Whether Tripo's or Meshy's shipped rig button runs the published models** (UniRig or anything else). Tripo's own blog stopped short of claiming product integration; Meshy documents behavior, never method. Anything World's method is entirely proprietary/undocumented.
- **License of the released checkpoints and datasets on HuggingFace** (UniRig weights, Rig-XL, Articulation-XL2.0): repos state code licenses, but per-asset training-data clearance and model-weight license terms were not stated in what I fetched. Objaverse-XL contains mixed-license content; derivative-work status of trained weights is legally untested.
- **Make-It-Animatable's runtime comparisons are its authors' own measurements** of competitors (including commercial tools); no neutral third-party benchmark of the 2024–26 systems was found.
- **HumanRig and RigAnything** were only surface-verified (titles/arXiv IDs seen in search results); I did not check their licenses, datasets, or claims in depth. Same for the 2026-dated arXiv items surfaced in searches (Skin Tokens, Rigel3D, Auto-Connect, ARMO, RigAnyFace) — a fast-moving tail I did not vet.
- **MagicArticulate skinning code**: skeleton-generation inference code and weights are confirmed released; whether the functional-diffusion skinning module is runnable from the repo is unconfirmed.
- **Determinism of autoregressive riggers in practice** (seeded sampling, temperature defaults) — not verified against the actual UniRig/MagicArticulate inference code.
