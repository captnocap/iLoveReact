# Angle 10 — Validation & Bad-Weight Detection

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): The de-facto academic metric suite for skinning weights is exactly three measures — precision/recall of the influential-bone set, average L1 distance between weight vectors, and per-vertex deformation error under sampled poses. Verified in the primary text of RigNet (Xu, Zhou, Kalogerakis, Landreth, Singh, SIGGRAPH 2020), SkinningNet (Mosella-Montoro & Ruiz-Hidalgo, CVPR 2022), and HeterSkinNet (Pan et al., I3D 2021); NeuroSkinning (Liu et al., SIGGRAPH 2019) is the origin cited by all three. "Influential" is a hard threshold: weight > 1e-4 (stated identically in RigNet §6 and SkinningNet §4.3).
- Claim (high): Deformation-error protocols are concrete and small-angle: RigNet — "given a test shape, we generate 10 different random poses, and compute the average and max distance error over the mesh vertices" (reference vs predicted weights, reference skeleton for all methods). SkinningNet — 10 random poses, "all the joints in the skeleton are randomly rotated within a range of ±10 degrees", on meshes normalized to [-1,1]. HeterSkinNet — rotates ~30% of bones with normally-distributed angles. All report average (and usually max) Euclidean vertex displacement vs ground-truth-weight deformation.
- Claim (high): Reference numbers exist to calibrate against (normalized-mesh units, RigNetv1 dataset): RigNet paper Table 2 — BBW prec/rec/avgL1/avgDist/maxDist = 68.3%/77.6%/0.69/0.0061/0.055; GeoVoxel (Maya) 72.8%/75.1%/0.65/0.0057/0.049; NeuroSkinning 76.3%/74.7%/0.57/0.0053/0.043; RigNet 82.3%/80.8%/0.39/0.0041/0.032. SkinningNet Table 2/3 — NeuroSkinning 82.3/79.7/0.41, RigNet 82.3/80.8/0.39, SkinningNet 87.0/80.8/0.33; avg/max deformation 0.002843/0.2151, 0.002921/0.2246, 0.002288/0.1789.
- Claim (high): The papers themselves say precision/recall/L1 are insufficient. SkinningNet, verbatim: "the Precision, Recall and L1-norm metrics are not good enough to evaluate skinning weight predictions… the magnitude of the skinning weight is not taken into account… Two similar L1 errors can result on different deformation errors depending on whether the L1 error comes from small differences on each of the associated joints or it is concentrated on a single joint. For these reasons, the deformation error will be used for the rest of the experiments." Deformation error is the metric to trust.
- Claim (high): RigNet's CD/IoU family measures the *skeleton stage*, not weights: CD-J2J (symmetric Chamfer joint-to-joint), CD-J2B (joint-to-bone), CD-B2B (bone-to-bone as line segments), IoU/precision/recall of joints via Hungarian matching with tolerance = half the local shape diameter at each reference joint, plus TreeEditDist (min joint insertions/deletions/replacements). A validator confusing joint metrics with weight metrics is mixing pipeline stages.
- Claim (high): Structural sanity checks are normatively specified in glTF 2.0 and machine-checked by the Khronos glTF-Validator: joints/weights come in VEC4 sets of 4 ("The number of joints that influence one vertex is limited to 4 per set"); "The joint weights for each vertex MUST NOT be negative"; float weights "linear sum SHOULD be as close as reasonably possible to 1.0"; quantized weights' pre-normalization sum "MUST be 255 or 65535"; "Unused joint values (i.e., joints with a weight of zero) SHOULD be set to zero"; joint indices MUST be in skin range. Validator message IDs: ACCESSOR_WEIGHTS_NON_NORMALIZED, ACCESSOR_JOINTS_USED_ZERO_WEIGHT.
- Claim (high): Engine influence caps: Unreal — "Default Bone Influences mode controls whether each vertex can be skinned by either 4 or 8 influences (depending on platform support)"; Unlimited Bone Influences mode exists but "in practice the maximum number of influences is capped to 12 because of the way Skeletal Mesh source data is stored" (official UE 5.x docs). Unity — SkinWeights enum None/OneBone/TwoBones/FourBones/Unlimited; QualitySettings.skinWeights clamps at runtime without changing mesh data. Epic's SIGGRAPH Asia 2023 paper states "in most real-time applications, the number of bones influencing a vertex is approximately 4-12."
- Claim (high): The Bounded Biharmonic Weights desiderata (Jacobson, Baran, Popović, Sorkine, SIGGRAPH 2011) double as a validator checklist: non-negativity, partition of unity, smoothness (minimized Laplacian/biharmonic energy), locality/sparsity, and no local maxima away from the handle. Cross-checked against "Robust Biharmonic Skinning Using Geometric Fields" (arXiv 2406.00238), which restates non-negativity, partition of unity, Lagrange property, smoothness.
- Claim (high): Epic Games' "Robust Skin Weights Transfer via Weight Inpainting" (Abdrashitov, Raichstat, Monsen, Hill, SIGGRAPH Asia 2023 TC) is the best documented industrial treatment of bad-weight geometry: confident region = closest point within D = 0.05 × bbox diagonal AND normal deviation ≤ 35°; canonical failure mode is leakage to spatially-near but geodesically-far parts (sleeve→torso, left pant leg→right leg); good infill = minimizing combined Dirichlet (−L) and Laplacian (LM⁻¹L) energies subject to sum-to-1; in practice unconstrained solves go only "slightly negative (≥ −0.03) so … we simply clamp the negative weights to 0 and re-normalize."
- Claim (high): Candy-wrapper collapse, collapsing elbow, and volume loss are *intrinsic LBS artifacts*, not weight bugs: described in Lewis, Cordner, Fong, "Pose Space Deformation" (SIGGRAPH 2000) and the SIGGRAPH 2014 course "Skinning: Real-time Shape Deformation" (Jacobson, Deng, Kavan, Lewis); mitigated by Dual Quaternion Skinning (Kavan et al., I3D 2007; TOG 2008) which introduces its own joint-bulging artifact, and by Optimized Centers of Rotation (Le & Hodgins, SIGGRAPH 2016). A validator must not flag these as bad weights — even perfect weights show them under LBS.
- Claim (medium): Pose-sweep ("ROM" — range of motion) testing is standard practitioner QA but published only informally: riggers key each joint through its range and eyeball deformation (Rigging Dojo tutorials, Tiny Phoenix's Maya ROM tool that keys selected channels four times each); Autodesk's own bakeDeformer command generates its training set "by moving the rig through a range of motion". No public, thresholded, automated skin-QA suite from a major studio was found — studio-internal skin QA appears to be proprietary/undocumented (GDC Vault has rigging tech-art talks, paywalled and unverified).
- Claim (medium): Volume tracking under pose sweep is well-founded: exact closed-mesh volume is a cheap divergence-theorem sum of signed tetrahedra (Zhang & Chen, ICIP 2001); Rohmer, Hahmann, Cani built exact volume-*restoring* skinning (CGF 2008; SCA 2009), which is direct evidence that LBS volume drift is real, measurable per pose, and correctable. Using volume ratio vs bind pose as an automated signal is my synthesis; no published pass/fail threshold exists.
- Claim (medium): Geodesic reasoning is the validated instrument for leakage detection: Maya's Geodesic Voxel Binding (Dionne & de Lasa, SCA 2013) exists precisely to bind through volume rather than Euclidean space on messy production meshes; SkinningNet's ablation shows Euclidean vertex-joint features vs geodesic ones balloon max deformation error 0.4473 → 0.1789 (tail deformed by spatially-near bone). Flagging influences that are geodesically far from their bone is therefore well-motivated — but as a *published validation metric* per-bone island connectivity does not exist; it is inference (labeled below).
- Claim (low): Max-vertex-velocity discontinuity across a pose sweep, and automated candy-wrapper cross-section detection at 90–180° twist, are useful detectors but I found no paper or engine doc specifying either as a metric; both are my inference from the artifact literature. Similarly, no source was found that publishes self-intersection *counts* as a skinning QA number — Implicit Skinning (Vaillant et al., SIGGRAPH 2013) documents self-penetration around flexing joints as the artifact class, and Epic's paper concedes interpenetration "for extreme poses" as a limitation, but neither turns it into a scored metric.

## Technical detail

Recipes, labeled [paper] / [practitioner] / [inference]:

**Ground-truth metrics (need a reference rig)** [paper — RigNet/SkinningNet/HeterSkinNet]
- Influential set I(v) = { b : w_b(v) > 1e-4 }. Precision = |I_pred ∩ I_gt| / |I_pred|, Recall = |I_pred ∩ I_gt| / |I_gt|, averaged over vertices.
- avg L1 = (1/V) Σ_v ‖w_pred(v) − w_gt(v)‖₁ (per-vertex weight vectors over all bones).
- Deformation error: sample P poses (RigNet: 10 random; SkinningNet: all joints uniform ±10°; HeterSkinNet: rotate 30% of bones, angles ~ Normal). E_avg = (1/PV) Σ_p Σ_v ‖x_pred(v,p) − x_gt(v,p)‖₂; also report E_max = max over vertices. Normalize the mesh first (RigNetv1 scales to [-1,1]) so numbers are comparable; good ≈ 0.002–0.004 avg, 0.03–0.22 max in that unit space.
- Caveat [paper]: RigNet's "ground truth" is artist-made and only artist-verified as "plausible" — these are similarity-to-artist metrics, not physical correctness.

**Structural checks (no ground truth)** [spec/engine-doc backed]
- Σ_b w_b(v) = 1 within ε (float: SHOULD ≈ 1.0; quantized u8/u16: raw sums MUST equal 255/65535). Non-negativity: hard fail (glTF MUST NOT). Expect small negative dips (≥ −0.03 per Epic) from Laplacian-type solvers → clamp to 0 and renormalize rather than reject.
- Influence count per vertex after pruning ≤ cap: 4 (glTF per-set / Unity FourBones / UE default), 8 (UE platform-dependent default), 12 (UE hard cap). Prune tiny weights then renormalize — pruning threshold is a real production knob (RigNet tuned Maya GeoVoxel with 3 max bones, 0.3 prune, 0.5 dropoff via holdout).
- No zero-weight vertices (Σ = 0 → vertex is unskinned/left at bind); joint indices in range; zero-weight slots carry joint index 0 (validator warns otherwise); reject NaN/Inf anywhere (the glTF validator flags non-finite accessor data; also trivial engineering hygiene) [medium on the exact validator message; the normalization/zero-joint messages are verified].
- Per-bone island connectivity [inference, motivated by GVB + SkinningNet ablation + Epic leakage figures]: for each bone b, take S_b(τ) = { v : w_b(v) > τ } (τ ≈ 1e-2), compute connected components in the mesh adjacency graph; flag components that don't contain a vertex within a geodesic radius of bone b's segment, and any influence whose geodesic distance to the bone is a large multiple of the component's median. Catches sleeve→torso / leg→leg leakage that Euclidean binding produces.
- Smoothness [paper-derived objective, inference as a metric]: per-bone Dirichlet energy E(w_b) = ½ w_bᵀ L w_b with cotangent Laplacian L (this exact quantity is what BBW minimizes and what Epic's inpainting minimizes in combination with LM⁻¹L). As a detector, compute per-triangle gradient magnitude ‖∇w_b‖ and flag outliers (z-score within the same mesh) — jagged weight transitions light up. No published absolute threshold exists.
- No-local-maxima check [paper-derived]: BBW lists local maxima away from handles as a defect of unconstrained biharmonic weights; discrete check — flag interior vertices that are strict local maxima of w_b whose star is far from bone b.

**Pose-sweep tests** [practitioner + inference]
- Sweep: for each joint, procedurally rotate each axis through its ROM (practitioner ROM keying; note academic protocols use ±10° — too gentle to expose artifacts; sweep to 90–180° bend/twist for QA).
- Volume: V = (1/6) |Σ_tri (p₀ × p₁) · p₂| (signed tetrahedra to origin; exact for closed meshes — Zhang & Chen 2001). Track V(pose)/V(bind) globally and per bone-region. Volume loss at bent/twisted joints is the collapsing-elbow/candy-wrapper signal. Threshold: unsourced — treat >5–10% regional loss as review-worthy [inference, explicitly unpublished].
- Candy-wrapper cross-section [inference]: sample planes perpendicular to the bone through the twist zone; intersect mesh → closed loop; compare loop area posed vs bind. Under LBS a 180° twist collapses the section toward a point *even with perfect weights* (averaging opposite rotations) — so run this test to characterize, not to fail weights.
- Stretched-edge detector [grounded in Epic's crease observation: "edges connecting the two vertices that are influenced by a different set of bones end up being stretched"]: per-edge length ratio len(pose)/len(bind); spikes localize hard influence-set boundaries (armpit/crotch class bugs). Cheap and effective; no published threshold.
- Self-intersections: count intersecting triangle pairs via BVH per sweep frame; rising counts at moderate angles indicate weight problems, at extreme angles indicate LBS/contact limits (Implicit Skinning motivation) [inference as a scored metric].
- Velocity discontinuity [pure inference]: finite-difference vertex trajectories across sweep frames; per-vertex max |Δx/Δθ| outliers correlate with disconnected islands and non-smooth fields.

**Distinguishing bad weights from intrinsic LBS artifacts** [synthesis, medium]
Run the identical pose sweep twice with the same weights: once LBS, once DQS (Kavan 2008 — same weights, rigid-motion blending). Artifact vanishes under DQS (twist collapse, volume loss) → intrinsic LBS, weights fine; artifact persists under both (leaked islands, jagged boundaries, wrong-limb motion) → weights. Expect DQS to *add* joint bulge (course notes) — don't count that against the weights either.

## What I'm unsure about

- No public studio document specifying a thresholded, automated skin-weight QA suite was found — Rigging Dojo/tech-art material covers workflow (normalize on, prune, mirror, ROM) but not scored validation. Studio-internal skin QA (Epic/Ubisoft/Bungie etc.) appears proprietary/undocumented; the GDC Vault talk "Technical Art Techniques: Character Rigging and Technical Animation" ([link](https://gdcvault.com/play/1012716/Technical-Art-Techniques-Character-Rigging)) is paywalled and its content unverified.
- All numeric pass/fail thresholds beyond spec normalization (volume-loss %, Dirichlet-energy outlier cutoffs, edge-stretch ratios, geodesic radius multiples) are unpublished — my recipes give relative/outlier formulations instead; treat any absolute number as a project-tuned constant.
- NeuroSkinning's metric definitions were confirmed via secondary sources (its GitHub, and RigNet/SkinningNet describing "as in [Liu et al. 2019]") rather than its own PDF; the ±10°/10-pose wording verified in SkinningNet could differ in detail from NeuroSkinning's original protocol.
- The Lewis et al. 2000 ACM DOI link is the standard one but was not fetched this session (paper identity triangulated via the SIGGRAPH 2014 course and multiple citing papers).
- Whether the glTF validator's NaN/Inf accessor check applies specifically to WEIGHTS_n accessors (vs. positions) was not verified message-by-message.
- I found no academic "rig quality" paper that scores candy-wrapper severity, self-intersection counts, or vertex-velocity discontinuity as standalone metrics; if such a paper exists (e.g., in deformation-transfer or rig-inversion literature), this angle missed it.
