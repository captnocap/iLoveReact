# Angle 01 — Bone Heat / Harmonic Diffusion (Pinocchio)

## Claims (with confidence)

- Claim (high): The bone-heat weight for bone i in Baran & Popović, "Automatic rigging and animation of 3D characters" (SIGGRAPH 2007 / ACM TOG 26(3), the "Pinocchio" system) is the equilibrium of a heat diffusion solved **on the mesh surface only**: `−Δwᵢ + Hwᵢ = Hpᵢ`, where Δ is the cotangent-formula discrete surface Laplacian (Meyer et al. 2003), pᵢⱼ = 1 iff bone i is the nearest bone to vertex j (else 0), and H is diagonal with Hⱼⱼ = c/d(j)² when the shortest segment from vertex j to its nearest bone lies entirely inside the character volume, and Hⱼⱼ = 0 otherwise. Verified verbatim against the paper PDF.
- Claim (high): The "nearest-visible-bone" test in the original paper is **not a ray cast**: Pinocchio checks segment containment in the volume using its precomputed adaptively-sampled signed distance field (octree, tolerance τ = 0.003 on a unit-cube-normalized model). Blender replaced this with a BVH ray cast (see below).
- Claim (high): c ≈ 0.22 makes surface-only equilibrium match volumetric heat equilibrium; Pinocchio nevertheless ships c = 1 ("corresponding to anisotropic heat diffusion") purely because "the results look more natural". When k bones tie for nearest, pⱼ = 1/k for each and Hⱼⱼ = kc/d(j)².
- Claim (high): The left-hand matrix (−Δ + H) is independent of the bone index, so the system is **factored once and back-substituted once per bone**. Pinocchio uses a sparse Cholesky factorization via the TAUCS library (citing Botsch et al. 2005 for the factor-once/multi-RHS pattern). Weights sum to 1 by construction: summing the equation over i gives (−Δ + H)Σwᵢ = H·1 ⇒ Σwᵢ = 1.
- Claim (high): Reported cost (2007, single thread, "1.73 MHz [sic — GHz] Intel Core Duo", 1 GB RAM): attachment takes 0.9 s at 19,001 verts, 1.9 s at 34,339, 3.2 s at 56,856; mean 1.8 s over 16 models (mean 33,224 verts). Attachment is a small fraction of total rig time (mean 31.3 s), which is dominated by distance-field construction.
- Claim (high): The paper itself documents an abandoned variant: pinning vertices "unambiguously attached to a single bone" to weight 1 gave negligible speedup and "introduced occasional artifacts" — a useful warning for implementers tempted by Dirichlet pinning.
- Claim (high): The paper's own stated quality limits: weights are material-oblivious (dress and armor both deform "rubbery"), and hips/shoulder-neck regions are worse than hand-tuned weights; LBS artifacts (knee/hip collapse) remain on scans.
- Claim (high): Blender's Automatic Weights is this exact algorithm — `source/blender/editors/armature/meshlaplacian.cc` opens its heat section with `/* From "Automatic Rigging and Animation of 3D Characters" Ilya Baran and Jovan Popovic, SIGGRAPH 2007 */` — with four verified deviations: (1) visibility by BVH ray cast from vertex toward the closest point on the bone segment instead of a distance-field containment test; (2) an extra normal term absent from the paper: effective distance = dist / (0.5·(cosθ + 1.001)), θ between the bone→vertex direction and the vertex normal, so back-facing vertices see the bone ~2000× farther; (3) a non-manifold-tolerant cotan Laplacian (per-edge cotan divided by the edge's face count instead of ×0.5, with an in-code comment admitting "it still needs to be verified that this is indeed the correct thing to do!"), rows scaled by inverse mixed Voronoi area (0.5/area), making the assembled matrix **non-symmetric**, hence (4) Eigen **SparseLU** (general LU, factor once, solve per bone) instead of Cholesky. Constants: `C_WEIGHT 1.0`, distances clamped ≥ 1e-4, and a post-solve soft cutoff ramp discarding weights below `WEIGHT_LIMIT_END 0.025` and ramping to `WEIGHT_LIMIT_START 0.05`.
- Claim (high): The infamous "Bone Heat Weighting: failed to find solution for one or more bones" message is raised exactly when `EIG_linear_solver_solve()` (Eigen SparseLU factor/solve) fails, i.e. the matrix is singular or contains non-finite entries. Root cause per Blender dev Brecht Van Lommel on issue #45493: "the algorithm does not explicitly handle connected components separately. They are all handled in a single matrix … if none of the vertices in a connected component are visible to any bones, then the matrix will not be invertible, and the solve will fail for all components." The issue was retitled "…(due to none of the vertices in an island being visible to any bones)" and registered as a **Known Issue** ("the algorithm has flaws. Users find it a regression"), still Open since 2015.
- Claim (high): The mechanism matches the math: an island where every vertex has Hⱼⱼ = 0 leaves a pure-Laplacian diagonal block whose null space contains the constant vector ⇒ singular. Degenerate geometry (duplicate/coincident vertices, zero-area triangles) produces infinite/NaN cotangents and zero mixed areas ⇒ factorization failure. Both mechanisms are visible in the code paths above.
- Claim (medium): Community-documented practical triggers, consistent with those mechanisms: duplicate vertices (Merge by Distance is the single most frequent fix), non-manifold geometry/holes/zero-area faces, mesh assembled from disconnected islands, bones fully enclosed by (or outside) the mesh, and extreme/unapplied scale. One 2026 community test of Blender 4.5 across 46 Mixamo characters had 8 hard failures, all multi-island/non-manifold characters (one with 385 islands); manifold single-mesh humanoids essentially never failed. (Community sources, not Blender-official; the official manual documents only that Automatic Weights uses the "bone heat" algorithm and "can often lead to armatures which do not deform child objects in ways you would want".)
- Claim (high): "Bone Glow: An Improved Method for the Assignment of Weights for Mesh Deformation" (Rich Wareham & Joan Lasenby, AMDO 2008, LNCS 5098, pp. 63–71, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-70517-8_7) is the direct published fix in this family: its abstract states it discusses bone heat, "provide[s] examples where it performs poorly", and develops a refinement that "performed as well as bone heat in simple cases while not suffering from bone heat's observed weaknesses." Secondary literature (HeterSkinNet, 2021) classifies bone glow as replacing heat diffusion's nearest-visible-bone term with an **illumination model** (bones as light emitters). The full formulation is paywalled (Springer) — I could not verify the exact equations from a primary copy; stated explicitly in "unsure" below.
- Claim (medium): Blender's cosine/normal distance modulation (deviation 2 above) is an undocumented in-code refinement in the same spirit as bone glow's one-sided illumination idea (surfaces facing away from a bone receive less influence), but I found no commit message or doc tying it to the Bone Glow paper — treat the connection as my inference, not fact.
- Claim (high): Alec Jacobson's SIGGRAPH 2014 course notes ("Skinning: Real-time Shape Deformation", Part II) give the standard modern reading: bone heat is unconstrained variational smoothing of a noisy nearest-visible-bone indicator — argmin Σⱼ ∫ ½‖∇wⱼ‖² + k(v)(wⱼ − ŵⱼ)² dV, discretized as (L + K)W = KŴ — whose documented weaknesses are: weights bounded in [0,1] and partition-of-unity hold, but **monotonicity is not guaranteed** ("intricate concavities and tunnels can lead to arbitrarily disconnected components in the raw weights"), **locality is the weakest quality** ("surface diffusion runs rampant" where no data term is active; Dirichlet energy drives weights toward a global average rather than zero), visibility is "a crude replacement for geodesic distance", and "diffusion over a surface is very different from diffusion through a volume". The notes credit bone heat's adoption in Maya and Blender to its avoidance of volumetric meshing.
- Claim (high): The failure modes drove two successor families: constrained-optimization weights (Bounded Biharmonic Weights, Jacobson, Baran, Popović, Sorkine, SIGGRAPH 2011 — non-negativity and locality enforced as constraints) and volumetric-geodesic weights (Dionne & de Lasa, "Geodesic voxel binding for production character meshes", SCA 2013 — explicitly built for meshes that are "non-manifold, non-watertight, have intersecting triangles, or comprise multiple connected components", i.e. exactly the inputs that kill bone heat; this shipped as Maya's Geodesic Voxel bind — the shipping claim is common knowledge I did not verify against Autodesk docs). A Blender-ecosystem equivalent is the commercial "Voxel Heat Diffuse Skinning" add-on (volumetric heat on a voxelization).
- Claim (high): The original Pinocchio implementation was released open source (library LGPL, demo MIT) and survives via GitHub mirrors (e.g. pmolodo/Pinocchio; stnoh/Pinocchio, a clone of the original mit.edu page). The seed pointer's project page people.csail.mit.edu/ibaran/ returns HTTP 200 with an **empty body** today (checked 2026-07-28); the historical autorig subpage 404s.

## Technical detail

Continuous statement (paper): treat the character volume as an insulated heat-conducting body; clamp bone i's temperature to 1, all other bones to 0; equilibrium surface temperature = weight. For tractability solve on the surface only, re-injecting volume information through H: `∂wᵢ/∂t = Δwᵢ + H(pᵢ − wᵢ) = 0` ⇒ **(−Δ + H) wᵢ = H pᵢ**.

Assembly for an n-vertex triangle mesh, b bones:
1. Cotan Laplacian: off-diagonal L_jk = (cot α + cot β)/2 for edge (j,k) (its two opposite angles); diagonal = −Σ row; normalize by per-vertex mixed area so Δ has units length⁻² (Blender: row-scale by 0.5/area_j, H added unscaled to the diagonal; the paper implies the same units argument — "Because Δ has units of length⁻², so must H").
2. Per vertex j: d(j) = distance to nearest bone **segment** (point-to-segment, not joint); visibility = segment j→nearest-bone inside volume (paper: distance-field query; Blender: BVH ray cast, offset 1e-5 from the surface, treating a hit as occlusion; Blender vertices with no adjacent face are treated as always visible). Hⱼⱼ = k·c/d(j)² over the k co-nearest visible bones (c = 1), else 0. pⱼ = 1/k on those bones, else 0.
3. Solve: one sparse factorization (SPD Cholesky if you assemble the symmetric form L_c + A·H and RHS A·H·pᵢ, where A = mass matrix — Pinocchio/TAUCS; general LU if you row-scale like Blender), then one back-substitution per bone. b right-hand sides, matrix n×n with ~7 nnz/row. Complexity in practice: near-linear per solve after factorization; ~1–3 s for 19k–57k vertices on 2007 hardware, trivially subsecond today.
4. Post-process (Blender only): weights < 0.025 dropped, 0.025–0.05 ramped, per-bone vertex groups written; optional x-mirror duplication.

Failure algebra: rows with Hⱼⱼ = 0 are pure Laplacian rows. Any connected component with all-zero H ⇒ singular block (constants in null space) ⇒ whole factorization fails ⇒ Blender's error. Coincident vertices/zero-area triangles ⇒ cot → ∞ / area → 0 ⇒ Inf/NaN entries ⇒ same error. Euclidean nearest-bone + visibility is the "leak" source even when the solve succeeds: geometrically close but geodesically distant parts (arm vs torso, coat sleeve vs arm bone) get wrong data terms, and diffusion happily spreads influence along the surface with nothing pulling it to zero far from all bones (Jacobson's locality critique).

Implementer guidance distilled from the sources: weld/clean before assembling (merge-by-distance, drop degenerate triangles); detect connected components and either solve per component or disable the visibility test for components with all-zero H (Brecht's proposed fix, never implemented in Blender); clamp d(j) away from 0; prefer the symmetric assembly + Cholesky; consider volumetric (voxel) diffusion if your inputs are production-grade multi-part meshes.

## What I'm unsure about

- **Bone Glow's exact equations.** The Springer PDF is paywalled and no open copy surfaced (checked Semantic Scholar, CiteSeerX, the authors' pages). I can source only the abstract plus secondary characterizations (bones as light emitters / illumination model replacing the binary nearest-visible-bone term). The precise per-bone irradiance integral, its visibility treatment, and the validation numbers are unverified here.
- **Whether Blender's cosine distance term derives from Bone Glow** or is an independent tweak — no commit archaeology was done; I found no documentation either way.
- **Maya's "Heat Map" bind internals.** Jacobson's notes assert bone-heat adoption in Maya; Autodesk does not publish the algorithm, and the Autodesk research page for geodesic voxel binding returned 403. Proprietary/undocumented — flagged, not speculated on.
- The paper's hardware line reads "1.73 MHz Intel Core Duo" in the PDF itself; I report it as the obvious GHz typo but note the discrepancy.
- Boundedness caveat: Jacobson states smoothed weights stay in [0,1] because the data does; with cotan Laplacians on obtuse/poor triangulations the discrete maximum principle can fail, so small overshoots are theoretically possible — I found no source discussing this specifically for bone heat (my inference, low confidence).
- Timing claims are 2007-era; no modern benchmark of the same code was located.
